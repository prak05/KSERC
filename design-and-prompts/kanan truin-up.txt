Public Hearing on the Petition  
 

5. The hearing on the petition for the Truing Up of Accounts for the year 2023-24 

was conducted at the Court Hall, Office of the Commission on 29-01-2025. The 

licensee was represented by Sri. Sachin Prabhu, Sri. R. Jayaraman and other 

officers of KDHPCL. Sri. Sachin Prabhu presented details on the petition and 

responded to the queries of the Commission.  



6. The main points made by the licensee are: 
 

a. The total number of consumers for 2023-24 is 17252 and the actual sale 

for the year is 507.90 lakh units. 

b. The energy purchased from KSEB Ltd is 566.94 lakh units and the KSEB 

Energy Feedback is 54.59 lakh units. The Gross energy in the system 

during the year 2023-24 is 621.53 lakh units  

c. The overall distribution loss for the year 2023-24 is 9.49% and the actual 

cost for the purchase of 566.94 lakh units is Rs.3370.52 lakh.  
 

d. The Commission while approving the norms in the Tariff Regulations 2021 

had approved the Operation and Maintenance Expense at Rs.233.34 lakh. 

The licensee in the petition has claimed total operation and maintenance 

expense of Rs.284.96 lakh. 

e. The Employee cost claimed for the year 2023-24 is higher than the norms 

approved by the Commission. The claim for the year is Rs.216.32 lakh as 

against the approved employee cost of Rs.174.96 lakh. 

f. The actual R&M expense for the year is Rs.49.74 lakh, which is higher 

than the approved R&M of Rs.43.88 lakh for the year.  

g. The A&G expenses claimed for the year is Rs.18.90 lakh as against the 

approved amount of 14.50 lakh. The major amounts booked are Rent 

Rates & Taxes, legal charges, Audit fees, Conveyance and vehicle hire 

charges etc 

h. The licensee during 2023-24 has made an asset addition of Rs.11.80 lakh, 

out of which Rs.6.34 lakh is towards replacement of energy meters. 

i. The licensee has claimed depreciation of Rs.15.24 lakh for 2023-24. 

j. The interest and finance charges claimed for 2023-24 of Rs. 71.46 lakh 

which includes interest on security deposits and the Bank Charges on 

Bank Guarantee/L.C to KSEB Ltd. 

k. An amount of Rs.18.54 lakh has been claimed as other debits which 

include Section 3(1) Duty under the Kerala Electricity Duty Act, periodical 

inspection charges and provision for doubtful debts. 

l. The licensee has claimed Rs.5.71 lakh as return on net fixed assets at the 

beginning of the year 2023-24 @ 5.50% 

m. The total expenditure incurred in 2023-24 is Rs. 3766.43 lakh. 

n. The Revenue from the sale of 507.90 lakh units in 2023-24 is                

Rs.3721.56 lakh.  

o. The Non-Tariff Income claimed by the licensee is Rs.109.63 lakh which is 

miscellaneous recoveries and interest on accumulated regulatory surplus. 

p. The total revenue for the year 2023-24 is Rs.3831.19 lakh. 

q. The petition for the truing up of accounts for the year 2023-24 is submitted 

with a revenue surplus of Rs.64.76 lakh. 
 



7. KSEB Ltd submitted the following comments on the petition;   

a) The licensee has failed to limit the distribution loss to the approved level.  
Hence the Commission may limit the energy requirement and power 
purchase cost by considering the approved distribution loss at 9.35% only 

b) The energy purchase cost of KDHPCL tally with the records of KSEBL, 
whereas there is difference in power purchase units. 

c) The licensee has claimed O&M expenses higher than the norms approved 
by the Commission. O&M expenses being controllable expenses, this may 
be approved only after prudence check. 

d) The true-up petition shows other income as Rs. 109.63 lakh, with a                  
Rs. 44.50 lakh deduction for provisions from the last three years, 
Commission may approve the other income based on prudence check. 

e) The Commission had approved Rs.62.00 lakh for reconductoring and 
voltage improvement in 2023-24, but the licensee has claimed only                 
Rs.11.80 lakh. No reconductoring work has been undertaken in the 
current or previous year to improve voltage and reduce line loss. 

f) Prudence check on assets and its corresponding depreciation is essential. 
g) The petitioner has included the cost of asset addition for 2023-24 in the 

calculation of "interest on normative loan." However, the licensee holds a 
substantial regulatory surplus of Rs. 1150.99 lakh as of 2022-23, and it is 
requested that the Hon'ble Commission consider utilizing this surplus for 
asset additions, thus not allowing interest on the asset addition. 
Additionally, the licensee has claimed Rs. 3.76 lakh for bank guarantee 
charges provided to KSEBL as per the Supreme Court's direction. 
However, the Commission has consistently disallowed such charges in 
past true-up orders, including those for 2017-18, 2019-20, and 2023-24, 
and it is requested that a similar stand be taken for the current true-up. 

h) The licensee calculated the interest on working capital with actual O&M, 
which needs to be corrected. 

i) The licensee has claimed Section 3(1) Electricity Duty which may be 
disallowed as was done in the previous Truing up orders. 

j) The Commission had specifically directed the licensee to conduct an age 
wise analysis of the receivables and to take steps to realise the 
receivables and to write off the receivables which are bad and 
uncollectable against the provision already available. With regard to the 
claim towards doubtful debts, similar stand may be taken. 

k) The petitioner has not considered the interest on security deposit with 
KSEBL as non-tariff income, whereas the Commission has consistently 
reckoned it as such. Additionally, the petitioner has incorrectly included 
interest on accumulated surplus after write back of excess provision for 
doubtful debts, which should be considered entirely as non-tariff income 
as per the regulation.

13. Distribution loss is a controllable parameter as per Tariff Regulations, 2021. 

Regulation 73(3) clearly specifies the methodology to be considered to account 

for any variation between the actual level of distribution loss and the approved 



level of the distribution loss. The relevant portions of the Regulation are as shown 

below: 
73. Distribution loss... 

................................... 

(3) Any variation between the actual level of distribution loss and the approved 
level of the distribution losses shall be dealt with, as part of the truing up of 
the respective financial year in the following manner:- 

i. if the actual distribution loss is higher than the approved level of 
distribution loss for any particular financial year of the control period, then the 
quantum of power purchase corresponding to the excess distribution loss for 
that financial year shall be disallowed at the average cost of power purchase for  
the respective financial year; 

ii. if the actual distribution loss is lower than the approved level of 
distribution loss for any particular financial year of the control period, then the 
savings in the power purchase cost corresponding to the difference in 
distribution loss for that financial year at the average cost of power purchase for 
the respective financial year, shall be shared between the distribution 
business/licensee and the consumers in the ratio of 2:1

the distribution loss to the approved level or better it. Accordingly, the

year 2023-24 is to be limited at the approved level of 9.35% and

The Commission notes that there has been severe fluctuations as far as the inflation 
indices are concerned. Further, due to the outbreak of COVID 19, and the overall 
depressing economic situation faced by all major industries in the country, the offtake 
of electricity during this period has considerably reduced and there is a glut as far as

However as of now, the economy seems to be on a path of revival and the increase in 
oil prices have also added to the overall inflation in the economy. Hence, the 
Commission feels that any data of past indices may not completely reflect the true

opinion that, for the purpose of ARR&ERC for the Control Period, it would be 
advisable to rely on historical inflation data for the previous four years. But, the actual 
escalation rates based on the actual inflation existing for each year of the Control

and would be a more reliable basis. Hence, the actual inflation indices will be allowed
