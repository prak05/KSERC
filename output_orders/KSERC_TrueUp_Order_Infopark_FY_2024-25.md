<h3>PART A: INTRODUCTION</h3>

<p><b>1.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;M/s Infopark, Kochi (hereinafter called the licensee or Infopark) is a Government of Kerala Undertaking, engaged in the development of infrastructure for information technology (IT) and information technology enabled services (ITeS). The licensee provides ready-to-occupy infrastructure, including power distribution, to IT companies in its Kakkanad and Cherthala campuses. Infopark, Cherthala was granted a distribution licence vide order dated 10-05-2010, with electricity distribution operations commencing from 01-07-2013. The Infopark Phase II campus at Kochi was granted a distribution licence vide order dated 18-10-2011. The licensee serves a mix of High Tension (HT) and Low Tension (LT) consumers, primarily in the industrial and commercial categories, within its licensed areas.</p>

<p><b>2.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;The Commission notified the KSERC (Terms and Conditions for Determination of Tariff) Regulations, 2021 (hereinafter referred to as the Tariff Regulations, 2021) for the five-year Control Period starting from the first day of April 2022 and ending on the thirty-first day of March 2027. In accordance with these regulations, the licensee filed the petition, dated 29-11-2025, for the truing up of its accounts for the financial year 2024-25. This petition is for the third year of the said Control Period. The Aggregate Revenue Requirement (ARR) and Expected Revenue from Charges (ERC) for the licensee for this period were approved by the Commission as part of the Multi-Year Tariff (MYT) order for the control period.</p>

<p><b>3.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;As per Regulation 67 of the Tariff Regulations, 2021, the licensee is required to submit a petition for the truing up of accounts for the previous financial year, based on audited accounts, within six months from the end of the financial year. The scope of the truing up is to verify the reasonableness of the expenses and revenues of the licensee for the financial year and to determine the revenue gap or surplus for that year. The Commission is required to carry out a prudence check of the submitted expenses and revenues to arrive at the final approved figures.</p>

<p><b>4.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;In the present petition, the licensee has claimed a revenue gap of Rs. 79.76 lakh for FY 2024-25. The Commission, in its MYT order, had approved an ARR of Rs. 56.56 lakh for the licensee for the same period. A comparison of the claims made in the present petition with the ARR approved by the Commission is tabulated below.</p>

<h3>HEARING ON THE PETITION</h3>

<p><b>7.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;The hearing on the petition was conducted at the Court Hall, Office of the Commission on DD-MM-YYYY. Sri. Vijayan V R, Dy. General Manager (Projects), and other officials represented the petitioner. They presented the key aspects of the petition and responded to queries from the Commission. The main points submitted by the licensee during the presentation are summarized below.</p>

<p><b>8.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;<b>Energy Sales:</b> The licensee submitted that the actual energy sales for FY 2024-25 were 137.61 Lakh Units (LU), a significant increase of 59.29% over the 86.39 LU approved in the ARR. This growth was attributed to the induction of new IT companies in the distribution area and a normal increase in energy consumption by existing consumers, reflecting a continued recovery and expansion of business activities post-pandemic.</p>

<p><b>9.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;<b>Distribution Loss:</b> The licensee reported achieving an overall distribution loss of 2.86% for FY 2024-25, which is lower than the 3.15% loss recorded in the previous year and significantly better than the Commission-approved target of 4.23%. It was highlighted that while the loss in the Kakkanad area was a low 2.41%, the Cherthala area continued to have a high loss of 10.49%, although this was an improvement over the previous year's 12.22%.</p>

<p><b>10.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;<b>Power Purchase Cost:</b> A total of 139.93 LU was purchased from KSEB Ltd at a cost of Rs. 1023.94 lakh. The licensee stated that the increase in power purchase quantum was directly correlated with the increase in energy sales. No solar power was injected into the grid during the period, as all solar generation was utilized for internal requirements.</p>

<p><b>11.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;<b>Operation & Maintenance (O&M) Expenses:</b> The licensee claimed total O&M expenses of Rs. 98.22 lakh (Employee Cost: Rs. 29.86 lakh, R&M: Rs. 58.30 lakh, A&G: Rs. 10.06 lakh). This was substantially higher than the MYT norm of Rs. 56.56 lakh. The licensee justified the excess claim by citing increased salary costs, a 7% hike in the outsourced O&M contract charges determined through competitive bidding, and one-time R&M expenditures, including the replacement of an EHT bushing for a 220kV transformer.</p>

<p><b>12.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;<b>Capital Expenditure and Depreciation:</b> The licensee stated that there was no asset addition during the financial year 2024-25. Accordingly, the depreciation of Rs. 47.21 lakh was claimed on the existing asset base, consistent with the previous year and in line with the provisions of the Tariff Regulations, 2021.</p>

<p><b>13.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;<b>Interest and Finance Charges:</b> A total of Rs. 136.60 lakh was claimed under this head, comprising Rs. 56.33 lakh as interest on normative loan, Rs. 8.89 lakh as interest on working capital, Rs. 9.13 lakh towards interest on consumer security deposits and bank charges, and Rs. 62.26 lakh as carrying cost for past revenue gaps.</p>

<p><b>14.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;<b>Revenue and Revenue Gap:</b> The licensee reported total revenue of Rs. 1262.32 lakh, comprising Rs. 1187.62 lakh from the sale of power, Rs. 64.46 lakh from wheeling charges, and Rs. 10.24 lakh as non-tariff income. Against a total claimed expenditure of Rs. 1342.09 lakh, the licensee petitioned for the approval of a revenue gap of Rs. 79.77 lakh for FY 2024-25.</p>

<p><b>15.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;<b>Comments of KSEB Ltd:</b> KSEB Ltd, the respondent, vide its letter dated DD-MM-YYYY, submitted its comments on the petition. KSEB Ltd confirmed that the energy purchase units and the corresponding power purchase cost claimed by Infopark for its Cherthala and Kakkanad campuses tallied with their records. They commended the licensee for achieving a distribution loss lower than the approved target and recommended that the benefit of this efficiency be shared as per regulations. However, KSEB Ltd pointed out that the claimed O&M expenses were significantly higher than the normative ceiling specified in the Tariff Regulations and submitted that such controllable expenses should be disallowed after a prudence check by the Commission.</p>

<h3>Energy Requirement and Distribution Loss</h3>

<p><b>21.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;The total energy input into the distribution system for the year 2024-25 is 242.68 Lakh Units. This comprises energy purchased from KSEB Ltd, as the licensee has reported no solar power injection to the grid during the period. A portion of this energy input is wheeled through the licensee's network to M/s Smart City. The distribution loss represents the difference between the total energy input into the system and the total energy billed to consumers, including both direct sales and wheeled energy. For the financial year 2024-25, the licensee has claimed an actual distribution loss of 2.86%.</p>

<p><b>22.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;In its Multi-Year Tariff (MYT) Order for the control period, the Commission had approved a distribution loss trajectory for the licensee. For the financial year 2024-25, the approved target for distribution loss was set at 4.23%. The licensee has achieved an actual distribution loss of 2.86%, which is significantly lower than the approved target. This achievement represents an efficiency gain, the benefits of which are to be shared between the licensee and the consumers as per the prevailing regulations.</p>

<p><b>23.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;Distribution loss is a controllable parameter as per the KSERC (Terms and Conditions for Determination of Tariff) Regulations, 2021. Regulation 73(3) specifies the methodology for treating any variation between the actual and approved levels of distribution loss during the truing-up process. The relevant provision is as follows:</p>

<blockquote><b>73. Distribution loss...</b><br/>(3) Any variation between the actual level of distribution losses and the approved level of distribution losses shall be dealt with, as part of the truing up of the respective financial year in the following manner:-<br/>
i. if the actual distribution loss is higher than the approved level of distribution loss for any particular financial year of the control period, then the quantum of power purchase corresponding to the excess distribution loss for that financial year, shall be disallowed at the average cost of power purchase for the respective financial year;<br/>
ii. if the actual distribution loss is lower than the approved level of distribution loss for any particular financial year of the control period, then the savings in the power purchase cost corresponding to the difference in distribution loss for that financial year at the average cost of power purchase for the respective financial year, shall be shared between the distribution business/licensee and the consumers in the ratio of 2:1.</blockquote>

<p><b>24.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;The Commission has analyzed the licensee's performance against the approved norm. The licensee has successfully reduced its distribution loss to a level below the target set by the Commission, which is a commendable achievement. The financial impact of this efficiency gain is calculated based on the quantum of energy saved and the average power purchase cost (APPC) for the year. <p><b>25.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;As per Regulation 73, the reduction in distribution loss is an efficiency parameter. The financial benefit arising from this efficiency, amounting to Rs. 27.63 Lakhs, must be shared between the licensee and the consumers in the ratio of 2:1. Accordingly, an amount of Rs. 18.42 Lakhs (two-thirds of the gain) is to be retained by the licensee, and Rs. 9.21 Lakhs (one-third of the gain) is to be passed on to the consumers. The licensee's share of the gain is recognized as an additional approved expenditure under the Power Purchase Cost head.</p>

<p class="commission-decision"><b>26.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;Considering that the licensee has achieved a distribution loss of 2.86%, which is better than the approved target of 4.23%, the Commission approves the sharing of the resultant efficiency gain of Rs. 27.63 Lakhs in the ratio of 2:1 between the licensee and consumers. The Commission allows the licensee to retain Rs. 18.42 Lakhs as its share of the efficiency gain.</p>

<h3>Power Purchase Cost</h3>

<p><b>27.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;The primary source of power for M/s Infopark is the Kerala State Electricity Board Ltd (KSEB Ltd). The licensee purchases power at various voltage levels to cater to its consumers in the Kakkanad and Cherthala areas. The cost of power purchase is the single largest component of the Aggregate Revenue Requirement (ARR) and is treated as a pass-through expense, subject to prudence checks on aspects like distribution loss and adherence to merit order dispatch principles where applicable.</p>

<p><b>28.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;In the petition for Truing Up of Accounts for FY 2024-25, the licensee has claimed a total Power Purchase Cost of Rs. 1023.94 Lakhs for the purchase of 139.93 Lakh Units of energy from KSEB Ltd. This corresponds to an average power purchase cost of Rs. 7.31 per unit. The licensee has also included a prior period expense related to the settlement of solar power drawn during 2023-24 in its claim.</p>

<p><b>29.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;The Commission has examined the details of power purchase bills and the reconciliation statement provided by the licensee. The quantum of energy purchased is consistent with the approved sales and the actual distribution loss level. The increase in power purchase cost compared to previous years is directly attributable to the significant growth in energy sales, which reflects increased economic activity within the Infopark campuses.</p>

<p><b>30.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;As per the Tariff Regulations, 2021, the cost of power purchase is a pass-through expense, subject to the sharing of gains or losses on account of controllable factors like distribution loss. As analyzed in the preceding section, the licensee has achieved an efficiency gain by maintaining distribution loss below the target level. The licensee's share of this gain, amounting to Rs. 18.42 Lakhs, is to be factored into the final approved power purchase cost.</p>

<h3>Power Purchase Cost</h3>
<p><b>83.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;The licensee has claimed a Power Purchase Cost of Rs. 1023.94 Lakhs for FY 2024-25. This cost pertains to the energy procured from KSEB Ltd to meet the demand of its consumers and for wheeling purposes. The primary driver for this cost is the total quantum of energy purchased and the applicable average power purchase cost (APPC).</p>
<p><b>84.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;As per Regulation 73(3) of the KSERC Tariff Regulations, 2021, any efficiency gain or loss on account of deviation in distribution loss from the approved target is to be shared between the licensee and the consumers. The Commission had approved a distribution loss target of 4.23% for the licensee for FY 2024-25. The licensee has achieved an actual distribution loss of 2.86%, which is significantly lower than the target, resulting in an efficiency gain.</p>
<p><b>85.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;The Commission has computed the financial impact of this efficiency gain. The reduction in loss from the target of 10.73 Lakh units to the actual of 6.95 Lakh units results in a saving of 3.78 Lakh units. Valued at the APPC of Rs. 7.31 per unit, this translates to a total financial saving of Rs. 27.63 Lakhs. As per the regulations, this gain is to be shared in the ratio of 2:1 between the licensee and the consumers. Accordingly, two-thirds of the gain, amounting to Rs. 18.42 Lakhs, is to be passed on to the licensee.</p>
<p class="commission-decision"><b>86.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;Therefore, the Commission approves an additional amount of Rs. 18.42 Lakhs over and above the claimed cost, representing the licensee's share of efficiency gain. Accordingly, the Commission hereby approves a total Power Purchase Cost of Rs. 1042.36 Lakhs (Rs. 1023.94 Lakhs claimed + Rs. 18.42 Lakhs gain) for the financial year 2024-25.</p>

<h3>Operation & Maintenance (O&M) Expenses</h3>
<p><b>87.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;Operation and Maintenance (O&M) expenses are controllable expenses comprising Employee Costs, Repair & Maintenance (R&M) Expenses, and Administrative & General (A&G) Expenses. The Commission specifies normative ceilings for these expenses for each year of the Control Period in the Tariff Regulations. Any claim exceeding these norms requires specific justification of being uncontrollable or a one-time expense, subject to prudence check.</p>

<h4>Employee Expenses</h4>
<p><b>88.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;The licensee has claimed Employee Expenses of Rs. 29.86 Lakhs for FY 2024-25. This claim includes salaries, allowances, and other benefits for the employees engaged in the electricity distribution business of Infopark.</p>
<p><b>89.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;The Commission has specified a normative ceiling for Employee Expenses for FY 2024-25 at Rs. 25.04 Lakhs as per the MYT order. The licensee's claim of Rs. 29.86 Lakhs exceeds this approved norm by Rs. 4.82 Lakhs. The licensee has not provided sufficient justification to treat this excess expenditure as uncontrollable. Adhering to the established regulatory framework is essential for maintaining tariff discipline. As a precedent, in the Truing-Up Order for FY 2022-23 (OP 82/2023), the Commission had similarly disallowed the excess claim over the approved norm.</p>
<p class="commission-decision"><b>90.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;In view of the above and as per the provisions of the Tariff Regulations, the Commission disallows the excess claim of Rs. 4.82 Lakhs. Accordingly, the Commission hereby approves Employee Expenses of Rs. 25.04 Lakhs for the financial year 2024-25.</p>

<h4>Repair & Maintenance (R&M) Expenses</h4>
<p><b>91.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;The licensee has claimed an amount of Rs. 58.30 Lakhs towards Repair and Maintenance (R&M) Expenses for the year 2024-25. These expenses are incurred for the upkeep of the distribution network, including transformers, switchgears, and other associated assets to ensure reliable power supply.</p>
<p><b>92.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;The approved MYT norm for R&M Expenses for the year is Rs. 26.41 Lakhs. The licensee's claim is substantially higher than the norm by Rs. 31.89 Lakhs. While the petition mentions certain one-time expenses, the justification provided is not sufficient to warrant approval of the entire excess amount beyond the normative ceiling for controllable expenses. The Commission, in its order for FY 2022-23 (OP 82/2023), had also undertaken a similar prudence check and disallowed expenses claimed above the approved norms.</p>
<p class="commission-decision"><b>93.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;After due consideration, the Commission finds that the claim exceeding the normative level is not admissible. Accordingly, the Commission hereby approves R&M Expenses of Rs. 26.41 Lakhs, in line with the approved norm for FY 2024-25.</p>

<h4>Administrative & General (A&G) Expenses</h4>
<p><b>94.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;The licensee has claimed Administrative and General (A&G) Expenses of Rs. 10.06 Lakhs for FY 2024-25. These expenses include insurance, audit fees, legal charges, and other office-related expenditures necessary for the operation of the distribution business.</p>
<p><b>95.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;The Commission has specified a normative ceiling for A&G Expenses for FY 2024-25 at Rs. 5.11 Lakhs. The claim of Rs. 10.06 Lakhs exceeds this norm by Rs. 4.95 Lakhs. The licensee has not substantiated the excess claim with reasons that would qualify it as uncontrollable or a one-time expenditure eligible for separate approval. Consistent with its approach in the previous Truing-Up Order (OP 82/2023), the Commission holds that adherence to norms for controllable expenses is paramount.</p>
<p class="commission-decision"><b>96.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;Therefore, the Commission disallows the excess claim of Rs. 4.95 Lakhs. The Commission hereby approves A&G Expenses of Rs. 5.11 Lakhs for the financial year 2024-25.</p>

<h3>Depreciation</h3>
<p><b>97.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;The licensee has claimed a depreciation of Rs. 47.21 Lakhs for the financial year 2024-25. Depreciation is a non-cash expense that represents the reduction in the value of assets over their useful life and is allowed as a pass-through in the ARR to enable the licensee to recover the cost of capital assets.</p>
<p><b>98.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;The depreciation has been calculated based on the Straight-Line Method at the rates specified in the KSERC Tariff Regulations, 2021. The Commission has examined the opening Gross Fixed Assets (GFA), the rates of depreciation applied for different asset classes, and the computation methodology. The claim is found to be in line with the regulatory provisions, and no new assets were added during the year.</p>
<p class="commission-decision"><b>99.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;The Commission has verified the calculations and finds the claim to be in order. Accordingly, the Commission hereby approves the depreciation of Rs. 47.21 Lakhs for the year 2024-25 as claimed by the licensee.</p>

<h3>Interest and Finance Charges</h3>
<p><b>100.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;The licensee has claimed a total of Rs. 74.35 Lakhs towards Interest and Finance Charges, which includes Interest on Normative Long-Term Loan (Rs. 56.33 Lakhs), Interest on Working Capital (Rs. 8.89 Lakhs), and Interest on Consumer Security Deposits (Rs. 9.13 Lakhs).</p>

<h4>Interest on Normative Long-Term Loan</h4>
<p><b>101.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;The licensee has claimed Rs. 56.33 Lakhs as interest on the normative loan. As per the regulations, in the absence of actual loans, interest is allowed on a normative loan base. The Commission has computed the allowable interest based on the approved opening normative loan, repayments, and the applicable interest rate (SBI EBLR). Based on the precedent from FY 2022-23, where Rs. 48.36 Lakhs was approved, and allowing for a reasonable escalation, the Commission finds a portion of the claim to be excessive.</p>
<p class="commission-decision"><b>102.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;After prudence check and re-computation based on regulatory principles, the Commission hereby approves an amount of Rs. 55.61 Lakhs towards Interest on Normative Long-Term Loan for FY 2024-25, disallowing the excess claim of Rs. 0.72 Lakhs.</p>

<h4>Interest on Working Capital</h4>
<p><b>103.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;The licensee has claimed Rs. 8.89 Lakhs as Interest on Working Capital. Regulation 32 of the Tariff Regulations, 2021, specifies the methodology for computing the normative working capital requirement. It includes one month of O&M expenses, maintenance spares, and two months of receivables, reduced by consumer security deposits.</p>
<p><b>104.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;The Commission has re-calculated the eligible Interest on Working Capital based on the approved components. The normative working capital is computed as Rs. 30.73 Lakhs [(O&M/12 of Rs. 4.71L) + (Spares of Rs. 8.48L) + (Receivables/6 of Rs. 17.53L)]. Applying the applicable interest rate of SBI EBLR + 2% (11.15%), the eligible interest works out to Rs. 3.43 Lakhs. The licensee's claim is significantly higher than the normative entitlement.</p>
<p class="commission-decision"><b>105.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;Accordingly, the Commission disallows the excess claim of Rs. 5.46 Lakhs and hereby approves Rs. 3.43 Lakhs as Interest on Working Capital for the year 2024-25.</p>

<h4>Interest on Consumer Security Deposits</h4>
<p><b>106.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;The licensee has claimed Rs. 9.13 Lakhs towards interest payable on security deposits held from consumers. This is a legitimate expense as per the regulations, and the licensee is obligated to pay interest to consumers on these deposits. The Commission has verified the amount based on the deposit balance and the applicable interest rate.</p>
<p class="commission-decision"><b>107.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;The claim is found to be in order and consistent with the regulatory requirements. The Commission hereby approves the claim of Rs. 9.13 Lakhs towards Interest on Consumer Security Deposits for FY 2024-25.</p>

<h3>Carrying Cost for Past Revenue Gaps</h3>
<p><b>108.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;The licensee has claimed a carrying cost of Rs. 62.26 Lakhs for the year 2024-25. This cost is allowed to compensate the licensee for the cost of financing the cumulative revenue gap approved in the Truing-Up orders of previous years. The claim is based on the opening cumulative gap and the applicable interest rate.</p>
<p><b>109.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;The Commission has verified the opening cumulative revenue gap from its previous orders and the interest rate applied for the calculation. The computation is found to be in accordance with the established regulatory practice of allowing financing cost on approved regulatory assets.</p>
<p class="commission-decision"><b>110.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;The claim is found to be accurate and justified. Accordingly, the Commission hereby approves the Carrying Cost of Rs. 62.26 Lakhs for the financial year 2024-25.</p>

<h3>Return on Net Fixed Assets (RoNFA)</h3>
<p><b>111.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;The licensee has claimed an amount of Rs. 36.11 Lakhs as Return on Net Fixed Assets (RoNFA) for the year 2024-25. As per Regulation 28 of the Tariff Regulations, 2021, where the equity of the licensee is not clearly identifiable, a return is allowed on the Net Fixed Assets (NFA) at the beginning of the financial year.</p>
<p><b>112.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;The return is calculated at 5.50% on the opening NFA. The Commission has verified the opening NFA value from the approved fixed asset schedule and confirmed the application of the correct rate of return. The calculation submitted by the licensee is found to be consistent with the regulatory provisions.</p>
<p class="commission-decision"><b>113.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;The Commission finds the claim to be in order and hereby approves the Return on Net Fixed Assets of Rs. 36.11 Lakhs for the year 2024-25.</p>

<h3>Revenue Surplus/Gap for 2024-25</h3>
<p><b>115.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;As against the licensee's claimed revenue gap of Rs. 79.76 Lakhs for the year 2024-25, the Commission, after a detailed prudence check and analysis of all components of expenditure and revenue, has determined the approved revenue gap. <p style="margin-left:32px;"><b>a) Total approved expenditure is Rs. 1312.67 Lakhs.</b></p>
<p style="margin-left:32px;"><b>b) Total approved income is Rs. 1262.32 Lakhs.</b></p>
