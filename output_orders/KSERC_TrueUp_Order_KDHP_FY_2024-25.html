1. Introduction

1.  M/s Kanan Devan Hills Plantations Company Private Limited (KDHPCL), hereinafter referred to as the licensee or KDHP, is a corporate tea plantation based out of Munnar, Kerala. The licensee is also a licensed distributor of electricity in Munnar since 2007, serving over 16,600 consumers spread over a 240 square kilometer area. The Kerala State Electricity Regulatory Commission granted the distribution license to KDHPCL, which came into effect from July 1, 2007, and remains in force for 25 years unless revoked earlier in accordance with the provisions of the Electricity Act, 2003. The licensee is engaged in the business of distribution of electricity within its defined area of supply.

2.  The licensee has regularly submitted petitions for the approval of Aggregate Revenue Requirement (ARR), Expected Revenue from Charges (ERC), Year-End Truing Up Petitions, and other submissions as per the relevant Acts, Rules, and Regulations. The present application, filed on November 25, 2025, is for the Truing Up of Accounts for the financial year 2024-25, covering the period from 1st April 2024 to 31st March 2025. The Commission's Order on ARR&ERC for the control period, including the financial year 2024-25, was issued vide Order No. OP 41/2025 (Mid-Term Review).

3.  The KSERC (Terms and Conditions for Determination of Tariff) Regulations, 2021, govern the truing up process for distribution licensees. Regulation 3 outlines the scope and applicability of these regulations, while Regulations 67 and 68 specifically detail the methodology for truing up of accounts. These regulations mandate the Commission to review the actual performance of the licensee against the approved Annual Revenue Requirement (ARR) and Expected Revenue from Charges (ERC) for a given financial year. The truing up exercise ensures that any variances between the approved and actual costs and revenues are prudently examined and accounted for, thereby safeguarding consumer interests and ensuring the financial viability of the licensee.

2. Hearing on the Petition

7.  The hearing on the petition was conducted at the Court Hall, Office of the Commission on 27.02.2026. Suman Ghosh, Chief Financial Officer, represented the petitioner, M/s Kanan Devan Hills Plantations Company Private Limited (KDHPCL), and responded to the queries raised by the Commission. The main points presented by the licensee during the hearing, along with the clarifications provided in the petition, are summarized below.

8.  The licensee submitted that the actual energy sales for the financial year 2024-25 were 491.10 lakh units, at an average price of Rs.7.55 per unit. This is compared to the approved sales of 448.71 lakh units at Rs.7.29 per unit in the ARR&ERC order, and 507.95 lakh units at Rs.7.35 per unit in the previous year (2023-24). The licensee highlighted that the sales for the year were higher by Rs.434.81 lakhs compared to the approved ARR-ERC, primarily due to a Rs.125.64 lakhs increase in average revenue per unit and a Rs.309.17 lakhs increase from higher sales volume of 42.39 lakh units. The increase in per unit revenue is attributed to the difference in tariff based on which the ARR was drawn with the actual tariff in effect, and the higher sales are due to increased HT IA consumption, mainly at seasonal tea factories, and faster recovery in commercial segments from the Covid period.

9.  Regarding distribution loss, the licensee stated that the actual distribution loss for the year 2024-25 marginally increased to 9.52% from 9.49% in the previous year. This figure is slightly higher than the ARR-ERC approved level of 9.10%. The licensee acknowledged the increase and indicated ongoing efforts to manage and reduce these losses.

10.  For Power Purchase Cost, the licensee claimed a total of Rs.3434.48 lakhs for the year 2024-25. This corresponds to a rate of Rs.6.26 per unit purchased. The licensee noted that the power consumption in the licensed distribution area was lower compared to the previous year, mainly due to a decrease in consumption by HT consumers. However, the increase in the per unit rate of power purchase is attributable to tariff revisions during the period.

11.  The Employee Cost claimed by the licensee for the year 2024-25 is Rs.257.53 lakhs. The licensee explained that employee costs have increased by Rs.41.21 lakhs compared to the previous year and by Rs.74.78 lakhs compared to the amount approved by the Commission in the ARR&ERC order (Rs.182.75 lakhs). This increase is primarily due to the payment of wages at an increased rate, along with arrears for three months of the previous financial year, resulting in an additional Rs.27.21 lakhs in employee costs. The licensee also stated that there has been no allocation of Corporate Office Overheads except for employee benefits expense of Management Staff directly involved in the Electricity Distribution business.

12.  The licensee claimed Rs.51.17 lakhs towards Repair & Maintenance (R&M) expenses for the year 2024-25. The breakup of this expenditure includes Rs.0.93 lakhs for Plant & Machinery, Rs.0.35 lakhs for Buildings, Rs.38.64 lakhs for Lines & Cable Networks, Rs.4.08 lakhs for Vehicles, and Rs.7.17 lakhs for LT Lines, upkeeps, etc. The increase in expenditure for Lines & Cable Networks by Rs.2.64 lakhs is mainly on account of increased transformer rewinding costs. The licensee stated that the variance in vehicle upkeep is not material, and the reduction in LT line maintenance is due to lower breakdowns.

13.  The Administration & General (A&G) expenses claimed for the year 2024-25 amounted to Rs.23.30 lakhs. This includes expenses such as Rent, rates and taxes, telephone, postage (Rs.0.09 lakhs), Insurance (Rs.0.49 lakhs), Legal charges (Rs.4.44 lakhs), Audit fees (Rs.4.20 lakhs), Travelling, conveyance expenses (Rs.2.36 lakhs), Security arrangements (Rs.2.46 lakhs), Printing and stationery (Rs.1.34 lakhs), Electricity charges (Rs.0.35 lakhs), Licensee fee (Rs.1.12 lakhs), and Miscellaneous expenses (Rs.6.45 lakhs). The licensee attributed the increase in A&G expenses to various factors, including legal/professional fees for litigations, travel and accommodation, and increased printing costs.

14.  Regarding Capital Expenditure and asset additions, the licensee stated that the amount capitalized during the year 2024-25 was Rs.1.52 lakhs. This includes Rs.1.05 lakhs for IT Equipment and Rs.0.47 lakhs for Other assets. The licensee also referred to the previous year's asset addition of Rs.11.80 lakhs, which was met from regulatory surplus, and requested the Commission to consider current additions as normal asset additions via borrowed funds, allowing for depreciation, return on NFA, and other financial costs, subject to the final outcome from the Hon'ble Supreme Court of India.

15.  The licensee claimed Rs.14.50 lakhs for Depreciation, computed as per Regulation 27 of the Tariff Regulations, 2021, using straight-line method. For Interest and Finance Charges, a total of Rs.71.19 lakhs was claimed. This includes Rs.6.09 lakhs for Interest on Normative Loan, Rs.18.93 lakhs for Interest on Security Deposits (calculated at 6.75% p.a. on Rs.297.01 lakhs), Rs.38.64 lakhs for Interest on Working Capital (computed at EBLR + 2%), and other bank charges. The licensee also claimed Rs.24.89 lakhs for Other Debits, including Duty-III charges, periodical inspection charges, and provision for doubtful debts.

16.  The Commission considered the petition of the licensee for Truing Up of Accounts for the financial year 2024-25 along with the clarifications provided in the hearing and the additional clarifications submitted by the licensee. The analysis and decisions of the Commission as per the provisions of the Tariff Regulations, 2021, on the petition for Truing Up of Accounts for the year 2024-25 are detailed below.

Energy Sales and Consumer Mix

17.  The licensee, in its petition, provided area-wise details of the number of consumers and sales for the financial year 2024-25. The supply area of KDHPCL includes various consumer categories such as HT I A Industrial, HT I B Industrial, HT II General, HT III Agriculture, HT IV Commercial, LT I Domestic, LT IV Industries, LT V Agriculture, LT VI Non-Domestic, LT VII Commercial, and Public Lighting. The licensee has observed significant changes in sales patterns compared to the previous year and the approved ARR-ERC figures.

18.  For the financial year 2024-25, the licensee reported actual energy sales of 491.10 lakh units. This figure is higher than the 448.71 lakh units approved in the ARR-ERC order. However, it represents a decrease compared to the 507.95 lakh units sold in the previous year (2023-24). The licensee attributed the overall increase against the ARR-ERC approved sales to a higher average revenue realization of Rs.0.26 per unit and a higher sales volume of 42.39 lakh units. The decrease compared to the previous year was primarily due to reduced consumption by HT consumers, particularly seasonal tea factories, which saw a decline of 23 lakh units, partially offset by a 6 lakh unit increase in consumption by LT consumers due to increased commercial and domestic usage.

19.  A category-wise analysis of sales indicates that while HT consumption, especially from seasonal tea factories, experienced a decline, the LT consumer segments, including commercial and domestic, showed an increase in consumption. This shift in the consumer mix and consumption patterns reflects the recovery from the COVID-19 pandemic and specific operational dynamics within the licensee's service area. The licensee has provided detailed breakdowns of sales across all categories, demonstrating the varying trends in demand.

20.  The per-unit revenue realization for the year 2024-25 was Rs.7.55 per unit, which is higher than the Rs.7.29 per unit approved in the ARR-ERC order and Rs.7.35 per unit in the previous year. This increase in average revenue per unit is mainly attributable to the revisions in the Retail Supply Tariff (RST) that came into effect subsequent to the previous tariff revisions. The licensee's projected revenue in the ARR-ERC petition had estimated a moderate increase, but actual units sold and average revenue per unit exceeded these projections due to the factors mentioned.

21.  The Commission has thoroughly examined the details of energy sales and consumer mix provided by the licensee. After analyzing the actual sales figures, the category-wise consumption trends, and the reasons for variances, the Commission finds the reported energy sales to be in order.

The Commission hereby approves the actual energy sales of 491.10 lakh units for the purpose of Truing Up of Accounts for the financial year 2024-25, as claimed by the licensee.

Energy Requirement and Distribution Loss

11.  The total energy input into the distribution system for the year 2024-25 is 606.33 lakh units. Out of these total input units, 548.83 lakh units of energy were purchased from KSEB Ltd, and 57.50 lakh units of solar power were injected into the grid. The licensee's distribution network wheeled 0.00 lakh units of energy during the year. Corresponding to the sale of 491.10 lakh units, the distribution loss claimed by the licensee in the petition is 9.52%. This indicates the overall energy flow and losses within the distribution system operated by M/s KDHP.

12.  The Commission, in its Multi-Year Tariff (MYT) Order OP 41/2025 for the control period 2022-2027, had approved a target distribution loss of 9.07% for the financial year 2024-25. The licensee, in its Truing-Up petition, has reported an actual distribution loss of 9.52% for the same period. This actual loss is higher than the approved target, which necessitates a prudence check and potential disallowance as per regulatory provisions. The difference between the actual and approved loss percentages highlights a deviation from the efficiency parameters set by the Commission.

13.  Distribution loss is a controllable parameter as per the KSERC (Terms and Conditions for Determination of Tariff) Regulations, 2021. Regulation 73(3) clearly specifies the methodology to be considered to account for any variation between the actual level of distribution loss and the approved level of distribution loss. The relevant portions of the Regulation are as shown below:

Regulation 73: Distribution loss...
(3) Any variation between the actual level of distribution losses and the approved level of distribution losses shall be dealt with, as part of the truing up of the respective financial year in the following manner:-
i. if the actual distribution loss is higher than the approved level of distribution loss for any particular financial year of the control period, then the quantum of power purchase corresponding to the excess distribution loss for that financial year, shall be disallowed at the average cost of power purchase for the respective financial year;
ii. if the actual distribution loss is lower than the approved level of distribution loss for any particular financial year of the control period, then the savings in the power purchase cost corresponding to the difference in distribution loss for that financial year at the average cost of power purchase for the respective financial year, shall be shared between the distribution business/licensee and the consumers in the ratio of 2:1

14.  The Commission has examined the licensee's claim for distribution loss for FY 2024-25. The actual distribution loss reported by the licensee is 9.52%, which is higher than the approved target of 9.07%. The total energy input into the system was 606.33 lakh units. The actual energy loss in lakh units is 57.73, while the approved target energy loss would have been 54.74 lakh units (9.07% of 606.33 lakh units). This results in an excess distribution loss of 2.99 lakh units (57.73 - 54.74). The average power purchase cost for the year is Rs. 6.26 per unit. Therefore, the cost corresponding to the excess distribution loss to be disallowed is Rs. 18.72 lakh (2.99 lakh units Rs. 6.26/unit).

15.  The Commission notes that the licensee has failed to achieve the approved distribution loss target for the financial year 2024-25. While the licensee has made efforts to manage its distribution network, the reported actual loss indicates a need for further improvement in operational efficiency. The Commission reiterates that distribution loss is a controllable parameter, and any deviation from the approved target, especially an increase, will be subject to regulatory disallowance to ensure that consumers are not burdened by inefficiencies.

16.  The Commission, after careful consideration of the actual distribution loss reported by the licensee and in adherence to Regulation 73(3) of the Tariff Regulations, 2021, hereby disallows the power purchase cost corresponding to the excess distribution loss. This disallowance amounts to Rs. 18.72 lakh, which will be adjusted from the total power purchase cost claimed by the licensee for the financial year 2024-25. The licensee is directed to take proactive measures to reduce distribution losses in the upcoming years to meet the approved targets.

Power Purchase Cost

17.  The licensee has claimed a total power purchase cost of Rs. 3434.48 lakh for the financial year 2024-25. This cost is primarily incurred for the purchase of 548.83 lakh units of energy from KSEB Ltd, as well as accounting for 57.50 lakh units of solar power injected into the grid. The average power purchase cost claimed by the licensee is Rs. 6.26 per unit. The Commission notes that the licensee's operations rely heavily on grid supply, supplemented by renewable sources, to meet the demand of its consumers.

18.  The total power purchase cost claimed by the licensee for FY 2024-25 is Rs. 3434.48 lakh. This amount is lower than the MYT ARR approved power purchase cost of Rs. 3491.65 lakh. The licensee's petition provides a detailed breakdown of demand charges, excess demand charges, and energy costs. The average power purchase cost of Rs. 6.26 per unit reflects the prevailing tariff structure and the mix of energy sources utilized during the year. The Commission's review focuses on ensuring that these costs are prudently incurred and align with regulatory provisions.

19.  The Commission has analyzed the power purchase cost claimed by the licensee. While the overall claimed amount is lower than the MYT ARR approved figure, a specific disallowance is necessary due to the licensee's failure to meet the approved distribution loss target. As detailed in the Distribution Loss section, an excess loss of 2.99 lakh units was incurred. This excess loss directly impacts the power purchase cost, as the corresponding energy had to be procured but was not delivered to consumers due to inefficiencies in the distribution network. The Commission consistently emphasizes the importance of adhering to efficiency parameters to protect consumer interests.

20.  As per Regulation 73(3)(i) of the KSERC (Terms and Conditions for Determination of Tariff) Regulations, 2021, if the actual distribution loss is higher than the approved level, the quantum of power purchase corresponding to the excess distribution loss shall be disallowed at the average cost of power purchase for the respective financial year. This regulation ensures that any costs associated with avoidable losses are borne by the licensee and not passed on to the consumers. The Commission applies this principle rigorously during the truing-up process to maintain financial prudence and promote operational efficiency.

21.  The Commission, after examining the details of power purchase and considering the disallowance due to excess distribution loss, hereby approves a power purchase cost of Rs. 3415.76 lakh for the financial year 2024-25. This approved amount reflects the claimed cost adjusted for the penalty incurred due to higher-than-approved distribution losses. The licensee is advised to implement robust measures to control and reduce distribution losses in the future to avoid such disallowances.

Operation & Maintenance Expenses

22.  Operation and Maintenance (O&M) expenses are controllable expenses which include Employee Cost, Repair & Maintenance expenses, and Administrative & General expenses. These expenses are crucial for the efficient functioning and upkeep of the distribution network, ensuring reliable power supply to consumers. The Commission's regulatory framework aims to ensure that these costs are prudently incurred and reflect efficient operations.

23.  As per Regulation 80(2) of the KSERC (Terms and Conditions for Determination of Tariff) Regulations, 2021, the distribution licensees shall be allowed to recover O&M expenses as per the norms specified in Annexure 7 of the Regulations. These norms are established based on a detailed analysis of historical data, industry benchmarks, and projected inflation, providing a transparent and predictable framework for cost recovery. Any deviation from these norms requires specific justification and prudence check by the Commission.

This claimed amount is higher than the approved O&M norm of Rs. 243.72 lakh set by the Commission in the MYT ARR Order for the same period. 30.  The paragraph-1 of the Annexure-7 of the Tariff Regulations, 2021 specifies the inflation rate adopted for tariff determination for the Control Period from 2022-23 to 2026-27. The average escalation rate approved by the Commission for the Control Period from 2022-23 to 2026-27, based on the average of the 'consumer price index and wholesale price index', is 4.454%. However, the 'Note' to paragraph-1 of Annexure-7 permits that the actual inflation rates based on the actual inflation indices shall be adopted for approving the O&M expenses at the stage of truing up for the respective years. Since the actual inflation rate for 2024-25 is not explicitly provided in the petition, the Commission relies on the MYT ARR approved norms, which are deemed to incorporate the prudently escalated costs.

31.  The Note to paragraph-1 of Annexure-7 of the Tariff Regulations, 2021, states: "The Commission notes that there has been severe fluctuations as far as the inflation indices are concerned... Regulation 27: Depreciation.—
(1) The depreciation shall be computed on the historical cost of the asset and shall be allowed for the useful life of the asset as specified in Appendix-I to these regulations.

Regarding the 'Interest on accumulated surplus' of Rs.87.52 lakh, the licensee reiterates its earlier views that regulatory surplus does not generate real cash surplus for fund placement. However, Regulation 83 of the Tariff Regulations, 2021, specifically includes interest income earned from security deposits and normative interest income calculated based on accumulated regulatory surplus under the definition of Other Income. The Commission has historically approved notional interest on accumulated surplus based on the SBI fixed term interest rate. For FY 2024-25, the retail deposit interest rate of State Bank of India for a one-year tenure as on 1st April 2024 is 6.80% p.a. Accordingly, the interest on accumulated surplus has been computed at this rate.
