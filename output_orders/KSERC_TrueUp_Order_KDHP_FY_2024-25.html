1.  M/s Kanan Devan Hills Plantations Company Private Limited (KDHP), Munnar (hereinafter called the licensee or KDHP) is a corporate tea plantation based out of Munnar, Kerala. The Petitioner is also a licensed distributor of electricity in Munnar since 2007 and serves over 16,600 consumers spread over 240 square kilometer area. The licensee is engaged in the distribution of electricity to various consumer categories including industrial, commercial, domestic, and public lighting within its licensed area, ensuring reliable power supply for both its plantation operations and the wider community.

2.  The licensee has filed the present petition for truing up of accounts for the financial year 2024-25 as per the provisions of the KSERC (Terms and Conditions for Determination of Tariff) Regulations, 2021. This petition is for the third year of the Control Period (2022-2027), for which the Commission had issued its Mid-Term Review Order OP 41/2025. The licensee has been regularly submitting petitions for approval of Aggregate Revenue Requirement (ARR), Expected Revenue from Charges (ERC), Year-End Truing Up Petitions and other submissions as per the relevant Acts, Rules and Regulations.

3.  The Commission, in accordance with the KSERC (Terms and Conditions for Determination of Tariff) Regulations, 2021, is mandated to undertake the truing up of accounts. Regulation 3 outlines the scope of these regulations, while Regulation 67 specifies the process for truing up of accounts, requiring the licensee to submit a petition with audited financial statements. Regulation 68 details the procedure for truing up, including the examination of actual performance against approved forecasts and the treatment of any variances. This process ensures that the approved tariff accurately reflects the prudent costs incurred by the licensee and that any revenue gaps or surpluses are appropriately addressed.

Energy Sales and Consumer Mix

7.  The licensee, M/s KDHP, operates a distribution network serving a diverse consumer base across its licensed area in Munnar, Kerala. The consumer categories primarily include HT (High Tension) consumers such as industrial units (seasonal tea factories), and LT (Low Tension) consumers comprising domestic, commercial, and agricultural segments. The nature of the licensee's operations, particularly its reliance on seasonal tea factories, significantly influences its energy sales patterns and overall revenue realization. Understanding the dynamics of these consumer categories is crucial for assessing the licensee's performance and the prudence of its claims.

8.  For the financial year 2024-25, the licensee has reported actual energy sales of 491.10 lakh units. This figure represents a decrease compared to the 507.95 lakh units trued up for the previous year (2023-24). However, it is higher than the 448.71 lakh units approved in the ARR&ERC for the current year. The licensee attributes the decrease from the previous year primarily to lower consumption by HT consumers, specifically seasonal tea factories, which experienced significantly lower production during the year. This decline was partially offset by an increase in consumption by LT consumers, driven by growth in commercial and domestic segments in the region.

9.  The category-wise analysis of energy sales reveals a mixed trend. While HT consumption, particularly from seasonal tea factories, saw a reduction of 23 lakh units, LT consumption increased by 6 lakh units. This shift indicates a recovery in commercial and domestic segments from the post-COVID period, but also highlights the vulnerability of the licensee's sales to the performance of its major industrial consumers. The licensee's average revenue realization per unit for FY 2024-25 was Rs.7.55, an increase from Rs.7.35 per unit in the previous year and Rs.7.29 per unit approved in the ARR&ERC. This increase in per-unit revenue is mainly attributable to the Retail Supply Tariff (RST) revisions implemented subsequent to the previous tariff period.

10.  The Commission has carefully reviewed the energy sales data submitted by the licensee, taking into account the explanations provided for the variances. The impact of seasonal factors on HT industrial consumption and the growth in LT segments are duly noted. While the overall sales volume has seen a slight decline from the previous year's trued-up figures, the licensee has demonstrated an increase compared to the ARR-approved projections for the current year. The Commission finds the reported energy sales figures to be in line with the operational realities and market conditions faced by the licensee during the financial year.

11.  After analyzing the details and duly considering the recovery from the COVID-19 pandemic and the specific operational challenges faced by the licensee, the Commission hereby approves the actual energy sales of 491.10 lakh units as per petition for the purpose of Truing Up of Accounts for the financial year 2024-25. The number of consumers for the year 2024-25 is stated to be over 16,600, as per the licensee's submission.

12.  Distribution loss is a critical efficiency parameter for any electricity distribution licensee, representing the difference between the energy input into the system and the energy billed to consumers. For M/s KDHP, this loss encompasses technical losses due to energy dissipation in lines, transformers, and other equipment, as well as commercial losses arising from theft, metering inaccuracies, and billing errors across its extensive network in Munnar. Accurate measurement of energy input and output at various points in the distribution system is essential for determining the actual distribution loss and for implementing effective loss reduction strategies.

13.  The Commission, in its Mid-Term Performance Review (MPR) Order for the control period, had set an approved target distribution loss level for the financial year 2024-25. As per the licensee's petition, the actual distribution loss achieved for the year 2024-25 is 9.52%. This is slightly higher than the ARR-ERC approved level of 9.10% for the same period, as indicated in the licensee's submission. The licensee has provided details of its energy input and output, which form the basis for the calculation of this actual distribution loss percentage.

14.  Distribution loss is a controllable parameter as per the KSERC (Terms and Conditions for Determination of Tariff) Regulations, 2021. Regulation 73(3) clearly specifies the methodology to be considered to account for any variation between the actual level of distribution loss and the approved level of the distribution loss. The relevant portions of the Regulation are as shown below:

Regulation 73. Distribution loss...

(3) Any variation between the actual level of distribution losses and the approved level of distribution losses shall be dealt with, as part of the truing up of the respectivefinancial year in the following manner:-

i. if the actual distribution loss is higher than the approved level of distribution loss for any particular financial year of the control period, then the quantum of power purchase corresponding to the excess distribution loss for that financial year, shall be disallowed at the average cost of power purchase for the respective financial year;

ii. if the actual distribution loss is lower than the approved level of distribution loss for any particular financial year of the control period, then the savings in the power purchase cost corresponding to the difference in distribution loss for that financial year at the average cost of power purchase for the respective financial year, shall be shared between the distribution business/licensee and the consumers in the ratio of 2:1

15.  The Commission has carefully examined the distribution loss figures submitted by the licensee and compared them with the approved target. Based on the authoritative financial data provided, the actual distribution loss for the year is determined to be 5.77 lakh units, which is significantly lower than the approved target loss of 48.99 lakh units (corresponding to a target of 9.07%). This substantial reduction in distribution loss results in an efficiency gain of 43.22 lakh units. At an average power purchase cost (APPC) of Rs. 6.23 per unit, this translates to a total efficiency gain of Rs. 269.26 lakh. As per Regulation 73(3) of the Tariff Regulations, 2021, two-thirds of this efficiency gain, amounting to Rs. 179.51 lakh, is to be passed on to the licensee, thereby increasing the approved power purchase cost.

16.  Considering the significant efficiency gain achieved by the licensee in reducing distribution losses below the approved target, the Commission acknowledges the licensee's efforts in operational improvement. The sharing mechanism, as stipulated in the Tariff Regulations, ensures that both the licensee and consumers benefit from such efficiencies. The Commission, therefore, approves the distribution loss as determined, incorporating the efficiency gain into the power purchase cost for the financial year 2024-25.

Power purchase cost

17.  The licensee, M/s KDHP, primarily sources its power from the Kerala State Electricity Board Limited (KSEBL) grid. In addition to grid supply, the licensee also has captive power generation facilities, with some energy being fed back into the grid. For the financial year 2024-25, the licensee has reported a total energy purchase of 548.83 lakh units from KSEBL, along with 57.50 lakh units of feedback energy. The overall power purchase cost claimed reflects the charges for both demand and energy components, as well as any excess demand charges incurred during the year.

18.  The licensee has claimed a total power purchase cost of Rs. 3434.48 lakh for the financial year 2024-25, corresponding to the purchase of 548.83 lakh units. This results in an average power purchase cost of Rs. 6.26 per unit. In comparison, the ARR&ERC approved an average power purchase cost of Rs. 6.21 per unit for the same period. The claimed cost includes Rs. 509.84 lakh for demand charges, Rs. 30.61 lakh for excess demand charges, and Rs. 2924.64 lakh for energy charges, as detailed in the petition.

19.  The Commission has analyzed the claimed power purchase cost in detail. The increase in the per-unit rate of power purchase compared to the previous year is primarily attributable to tariff revisions implemented during the period. The licensee has also accounted for excess demand charges, which are subject to prudence check by the Commission. The overall quantum of power purchased is influenced by the energy sales to consumers and the distribution losses incurred in the network. The Commission notes the licensee's efforts to manage its power procurement efficiently.

20.  As per the KSERC (Terms and Conditions for Determination of Tariff) Regulations, 2021, the power purchase cost is a pass-through expense, subject to prudence check and adjustments for distribution loss performance. Regulation 73(3) specifies that if the actual distribution loss is higher than the approved level, the quantum of power purchase corresponding to the excess distribution loss shall be disallowed at the average cost of power purchase for the respective financial year. Conversely, if the actual distribution loss is lower, the savings are shared between the licensee and consumers.

21.  The Commission has carefully considered the claimed power purchase cost, taking into account the energy units purchased, the average power purchase rate, and the adjustments related to distribution loss efficiency. As detailed in the distribution loss section, an efficiency gain of Rs. 179.51 lakh has been identified and is to be passed on to the licensee. This adjustment increases the approved power purchase cost from the claimed amount. The Commission, after due diligence, approves the power purchase cost for the financial year 2024-25.

Operation & Maintenance expenses

22.  Operation and Maintenance (O&M) expenses are a significant component of the Aggregate Revenue Requirement (ARR) for any distribution licensee. These expenses are categorized as controllable factors and primarily include Employee Cost, Repair & Maintenance (R&M) Expenses, and Administrative & General (A&G) Expenses. The Commission's approach to approving O&M expenses is guided by the principle of allowing reasonable and prudent costs necessary for efficient operation while encouraging cost efficiency.

23.  As per Regulation 80(2) of the KSERC (Terms and Conditions for Determination of Tariff) Regulations, 2021, distribution licensees are allowed to recover O&M expenses based on the norms specified in Annexure 7 of the Regulations. These norms are established to provide a predictable framework for O&M cost recovery, ensuring that only efficient and justifiable expenses are passed on to consumers. The Regulations also provide for adjustments during the truing-up process to account for actual inflation and other uncontrollable factors, subject to prudence check by the Commission.

24.  For the financial year 2024-25, the Commission had approved a total O&M norm of Rs. 243.72 lakh in its ARR&ERC Order. However, the licensee, M/s KDHP, has claimed a total O&M expense of Rs. 308.14 lakh in its truing-up petition. This claimed amount is higher than the approved normative level, necessitating a detailed examination by the Commission to ascertain the prudence and admissibility of each component of the O&M expenses. 30.  The methodology adopted for specifying the Normative Operation & Maintenance (O&M) Costs for the 5-year Control Period (2022-27) involves an annual inflation/escalation rate. The Commission had fixed an annual escalation factor of 4.454% for the Control Period, based on the average increase in CPI and WPI indices from 2017-18 to 2020-21. This escalation rate is applied to the base year O&M expenses to arrive at the normative costs for each year of the control period. The approved MYT norm for Employee Cost for 2024-25, which is Rs. 182.75 lakh, already incorporates this inflation adjustment.

31.  Furthermore, the 'Note' to paragraph 1 of Annexure-7 of the Tariff Regulations 2021 explicitly permits that the actual escalation rates, based on the actual inflation indices, shall be adopted at the stage of truing up for the respective years. This provision allows the Commission to consider the real economic conditions and their impact on O&M expenses, ensuring that the approved costs reflect the true economic situation. This flexibility is crucial, especially when there are significant deviations between projected and actual inflation rates, or when specific uncontrollable expenses like wage revisions occur.

Regulation 27: Depreciation.—
