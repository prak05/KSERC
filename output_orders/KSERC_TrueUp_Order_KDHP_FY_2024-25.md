<h3>PART A: INTRODUCTION</h3>

<p><b>1.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;M/s Kanan Devan Hills Plantations Company Private Limited (hereinafter referred to as KDHPCL or the licensee) is a corporate tea plantation company based in Munnar, Kerala. The licensee was granted a license for electricity distribution by the Commission vide Order dated 15.05.2007. The licensee serves over 16,600 consumers, primarily comprising its own residential colonies, commercial establishments, and tea factories, spread across a licensed area of approximately 240 square kilometers in the Munnar region. The distribution network consists of both High Tension (HT) and Low Tension (LT) lines, catering to a diverse mix of consumers including domestic, commercial, industrial (tea factories), and public lighting.</p>

<p><b>2.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;The licensee filed the petition for Truing Up of Accounts for the financial year 2024-25 on 25.11.2025. This petition is for the third year of the fifth Control Period, which commenced on 01.04.2022 and ends on 31.03.2027. The tariff for the licensee for this Control Period was determined by the Commission as per the KSERC (Terms and Conditions for Determination of Tariff) Regulations, 2021 (hereinafter referred to as the Tariff Regulations, 2021). The licensee has been regularly submitting petitions for Aggregate Revenue Requirement (ARR), Expected Revenue from Charges (ERC), and Truing Up of Accounts as per the prevailing regulations.</p>

<p><b>3.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;As per Regulation 67 of the Tariff Regulations, 2021, the licensee is required to file a petition for the truing up of its accounts for the previous financial year, based on audited annual statements of accounts. The Commission is required to undertake a prudence check of the expenses and revenues claimed by the licensee and determine the revenue gap or surplus for the year. This process ensures that the costs passed on to consumers are reasonable and that the licensee operates efficiently. The present order is issued after a detailed examination of the petition, clarifications provided by the licensee, comments from the respondent KSEB Ltd, and proceedings of the public hearing.</p>

<h3>HEARING ON THE PETITION</h3>

<p><b>7.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;The hearing on the petition was conducted at the Court Hall, Office of the Commission, Thiruvananthapuram, on 15.04.2026. Sri. Suman Ghosh, Chief Financial Officer, represented the petitioner and made a detailed presentation on the Truing Up petition for FY 2024-25. The respondent, KSEB Ltd, was represented by its officials. The main points presented by the licensee and the comments from the respondent are summarized below.</p>

<p><b>8.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;The licensee submitted that the actual energy sales for FY 2024-25 were 491.10 lakh units against the approved 448.71 lakh units. The increase in sales was attributed to higher consumption by HT-IA (tea factories) consumers and a faster-than-anticipated recovery in commercial segments post-COVID. The revenue from sale of power was Rs. 3707.46 lakh.</p>

<p><b>9.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;Regarding distribution loss, the licensee stated that the actual loss for the year was 9.52%. <p><b>10.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;The power purchase cost claimed for the year is Rs. 3434.48 lakh for the purchase of 548.83 lakh units from KSEB Ltd. The licensee stated that the increase in per-unit power purchase cost was due to tariff revisions by KSEB Ltd during the year.</p>

<p><b>11.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;On Operation & Maintenance (O&M) expenses, the licensee claimed a total of Rs. 332.00 lakh, comprising Rs. 257.53 lakh towards Employee Cost, Rs. 51.17 lakh for Repair & Maintenance, and Rs. 23.30 lakh for Administration & General expenses. <p><b>12.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;The licensee claimed depreciation of Rs. 14.50 lakh for the year, based on the rates specified in the Tariff Regulations, 2021. <p><b>13.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;The licensee claimed Other Debits of Rs. 24.89 lakh, primarily on account of Duty-III charges levied by the Government, and a provision for doubtful debts. They argued that Duty-III is a statutory levy and should be allowed as a pass-through expense.</p>

<p><b>14.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;KSEB Ltd, in its submission vide letter dated 10.04.2026, stated that the energy purchase units and cost claimed by KDHPCL tallied with their records. <p><b>15.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;The Commission sought clarifications from the licensee on the reasons for the steep increase in employee costs, the justification for the high distribution loss, and the basis for claiming provisions for doubtful debts. <h3>Energy Requirement and Distribution Loss</h3>

<p><b>22.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;The distribution loss is a critical parameter that reflects the technical and commercial efficiency of a distribution licensee. It represents the difference between the total energy input into the distribution system and the total energy billed to consumers. For M/s Kanan Devan Hills Plantations Company Private Limited (KDHP), the energy input primarily consists of power purchased from the Kerala State Electricity Board Limited (KSEB Ltd) grid, measured at various injection points.</p>

<p><b>23.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;In its petition for Truing Up of accounts for FY 2024-25, the licensee has claimed an actual distribution loss of 9.52%. This is computed based on a total energy input of 606.33 lakh units (548.83 lakh units purchased + 57.50 lakh units feedback) and total energy sales of 491.10 lakh units, resulting in a line loss of 57.73 lakh units. The Commission, in its Multi-Year Tariff (MYT) order for the control period, had established a target distribution loss trajectory for the licensee to incentivize efficiency improvements. For the financial year 2024-25, the approved target distribution loss was 3.35%.</p>

<p><b>24.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;The treatment of variation between actual and approved distribution loss is governed by the KSERC (Terms and Conditions for Determination of Tariff) Regulations, 2021. Regulation 73(3) specifies the methodology for sharing the gains from efficiency improvements or penalizing for performance below the target.</p>

<blockquote>
<b>Regulation 73. Distribution loss...</b><br/>
(3) Any variation between the actual level of distribution losses and the approved level of distribution losses shall be dealt with, as part of the truing up of the respective financial year in the following manner:-<br/>
i. if the actual distribution loss is higher than the approved level of distribution loss for any particular financial year of the control period, then the quantum of power purchase corresponding to the excess distribution loss for that financial year, shall be disallowed at the average cost of power purchase for the respective financial year;<br/>
ii. if the actual distribution loss is lower than the approved level of distribution loss for any particular financial year of the control period, then the savings in the power purchase cost corresponding to the difference in distribution loss for that financial year at the average cost of power purchase for the respective financial year, shall be shared between the distribution business/licensee and the consumers in the ratio of 2:1.
</blockquote>

<p><b>25.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;The Commission has analyzed the licensee's claim. The actual distribution loss of 9.52% is significantly higher than the approved target of 3.35%. This indicates a shortfall in achieving the operational efficiency targets set by the Commission. As per the regulation, the financial impact of this excess loss must be borne by the licensee and cannot be passed on to the consumers through tariffs. The calculation of the financial penalty is based on the quantum of excess energy loss and the average power purchase cost for the year.</p>

<p><b>26.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;The computation of the penalty for excess distribution loss is as follows: The total energy input into the system was 606.33 lakh units. The approved target loss is 3.35%, which corresponds to an allowable loss of 19.02 lakh units. The actual loss incurred by the licensee was 57.73 lakh units. Therefore, the excess distribution loss is 38.71 lakh units (57.73 - 19.02). The average power purchase cost (APPC) for the year is Rs. 6.26 per unit. The total financial penalty corresponding to the excess loss is Rs. 242.32 lakh (38.71 lakh units * Rs. 6.26/unit). This amount shall be disallowed from the power purchase cost claimed by the licensee.</p>

<h3>Power Purchase Cost</h3>

<p><b>28.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;The power purchase cost is the single largest component of the Aggregate Revenue Requirement (ARR) for a distribution licensee. M/s KDHP meets its entire electricity requirement by purchasing power from the KSEB Ltd grid at various voltage levels. The cost of this power is determined by the bulk supply tariff approved by the Commission for KSEB Ltd.</p>

<p><b>29.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;The licensee has claimed a total power purchase cost of Rs. 3434.48 lakh for the procurement of 548.83 lakh units of electricity during FY 2024-25. This translates to an average power purchase cost of Rs. 6.26 per unit. The licensee has submitted the monthly power purchase bills from KSEB Ltd as evidence for the claimed expenditure. The quantum of power purchased is directly related to the consumer demand and the level of distribution losses in the network.</p>

<p><b>30.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;As per the Tariff Regulations, the Commission shall allow the prudent cost of power purchase. This includes verifying the quantum of energy purchased against the energy sales and the approved distribution loss levels. Any cost incurred due to inefficiencies, such as excess distribution losses, is not considered a prudent expense and is disallowed from being passed through in tariffs.</p>

<p><b>31.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;The Commission has examined the claim of the licensee. The energy purchase units and the corresponding costs have been verified with the data from KSEB Ltd and found to be in order. However, as detailed in the preceding section on Distribution Loss, the licensee's operational performance was below the normative target. The Commission has calculated a penalty of Rs. 242.32 lakh for the excess distribution loss incurred during the year.</p>

The figures are found to be consistent with the actual energy sales and the applicable tariff schedule for the period. The revenue realization is in line with the regulatory framework.</p>

<p class="commission-decision"><b>92.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;The Commission hereby approves the Revenue from Sale of Power of Rs. 3707.46 lakh as reported by the licensee for the financial year 2024-25.</p>

<h3>Non-Tariff Income</h3>
<p><b>93.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;The licensee has accounted for a Non-Tariff Income of Rs. 124.07 lakh for FY 2024-25. This income includes revenue from sources other than the sale of power, such as delayed payment surcharges, meter rent, and interest on the accumulated regulatory surplus as per the Tariff Regulations, 2021.</p>

<p><b>94.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;The Commission has examined the components of the non-tariff income. The calculations are found to be in accordance with the provisions of the Tariff Regulations. This income serves to reduce the overall revenue requirement that needs to be collected from consumers through tariffs.</p>

<p class="commission-decision"><b>95.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;The Commission hereby approves the Non-Tariff Income of Rs. 124.07 lakh as claimed by the licensee for the financial year 2024-25.</p>
