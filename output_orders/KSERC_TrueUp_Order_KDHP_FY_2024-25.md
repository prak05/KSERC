<h3>PART A: INTRODUCTION AND ANALYSIS</h3>

<p><b>1.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;M/s Kanan Devan Hills Plantations Company Private Limited (hereinafter referred to as KDHPCL or the licensee) is a corporate tea plantation company based in Munnar, Kerala. The licensee was granted a license for electricity distribution by the Commission and has been operating as a distribution licensee since 2007. The licensed area of supply covers approximately 240 square kilometers, serving over 16,600 consumers. The consumer base primarily consists of its own tea estates and factories (HT-IA), residential colonies (LT-Domestic), and various commercial establishments within its operational area.</p>

<p><b>2.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;The licensee filed the petition for Truing Up of Accounts for the financial year 2024-25 on 25th November 2025. The Aggregate Revenue Requirement (ARR) and Expected Revenue from Charges (ERC) for the licensee for the fourth control period (FY 2022-23 to FY 2026-27) were approved by the Commission vide its order in OP No. 28/2022 dated 26.02.2024. The present petition for truing up of accounts for FY 2024-25 has been filed in accordance with the provisions of the said order and the extant regulations.</p>

<p><b>3.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;The truing up of accounts is a regulatory process governed by the Kerala State Electricity Regulatory Commission (Terms and Conditions for Determination of Tariff) Regulations, 2021 (hereinafter referred to as the Tariff Regulations, 2021). As per Regulation 14 of the Tariff Regulations, 2021, the Commission shall undertake the truing up of expenses and revenues of the licensee for each financial year of the Control Period based on the audited annual accounts. This process involves a prudence check of the actual expenditure incurred and revenue realized against the approved norms and projections, with any uncontrollable variations being adjusted and controllable variations being shared or disallowed as per the regulatory framework.</p>

<h3>Energy Requirement and Distribution Loss</h3>

<p><b>22.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;The licensee, M/s Kanan Devan Hills Plantations Company Private Limited (KDHP), operates a distribution network primarily serving its tea plantations, associated factories, and residential colonies in the Munnar region. The energy input into the system is sourced from the Kerala State Electricity Board Limited (KSEB Ltd). The determination of distribution loss involves accounting for the total energy injected into the network (input) and the total energy billed to consumers (output). Any difference is attributed to technical and commercial losses within the distribution system.</p>

<p><b>23.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;In the petition for Truing Up of accounts for FY 2024-25, the licensee has claimed an actual distribution loss of 9.52%. The Commission, in its Multi-Year Tariff (MYT) Order for the control period, had approved a target distribution loss of 9.07% for the licensee for FY 2024-25. A comparison of the approved target and the actual achievement is critical for assessing the operational efficiency of the licensee during the year.</p>

<p><b>24.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;Distribution loss is a controllable parameter as per the KSERC (Terms and Conditions for Determination of Tariff) Regulations, 2021. Regulation 73 of the said regulations specifies the mechanism for sharing of gains or losses on account of deviation from the approved loss levels. The relevant portion of the Regulation is as shown below:</p>

<blockquote>
    <b>73. Distribution loss...</b><br/>
    (3) Any variation between the actual level of distribution losses and the approved level of distribution losses shall be dealt with, as part of the truing up of the respective financial year in the following manner:-<br/>
    i. if the actual distribution loss is higher than the approved level of distribution loss for any particular financial year of the control period, then the quantum of power purchase corresponding to the excess distribution loss for that financial year, shall be disallowed at the average cost of power purchase for the respective financial year;<br/>
    ii. if the actual distribution loss is lower than the approved level of distribution loss for any particular financial year of the control period, then the savings in the power purchase cost corresponding to the difference in distribution loss for that financial year at the average cost of power purchase for the respective financial year, shall be shared between the distribution business/licensee and the consumers in the ratio of 2:1.
</blockquote>

<p><b>25.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;The Commission has examined the details of energy input and sales submitted by the licensee. The total energy purchased from KSEB Ltd was 548.83 lakh units. The total energy sold to consumers was 491.10 lakh units. This results in an actual distribution loss of 57.73 lakh units, which corresponds to a loss level of 9.52% of the total energy handled (including feedback units). The approved target for the year was 9.07%.</p>

<p><b>26.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;Since the actual distribution loss of 9.52% is higher than the approved target of 9.07%, the licensee has failed to meet the operational efficiency target set by the Commission. As per Regulation 73(3)(i), the cost of power purchase corresponding to the excess loss must be disallowed. The calculation of the penalty is detailed in the table below.</p>

<h3>Power Purchase Cost</h3>

<p><b>28.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;The primary source of power for the licensee's distribution business is the bulk supply received from KSEB Ltd at various injection points. The cost of this power is the single largest component of the licensee's Aggregate Revenue Requirement (ARR). The power purchase cost is determined by the tariff set by the Commission for KSEB Ltd and the quantum of energy drawn by the licensee.</p>

<p><b>29.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;For the financial year 2024-25, the licensee has claimed a total power purchase cost of Rs. 3434.48 lakh for the purchase of 548.83 lakh units of energy. This translates to an average power purchase cost of Rs. 6.26 per unit. The licensee has submitted the monthly power purchase bills from KSEB Ltd in support of its claim.</p>

<p><b>30.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;As per the Tariff Regulations, 2021, the prudent cost of power purchase is a pass-through expense in the ARR. The Commission has examined the power purchase bills and verified the quantum and cost of energy procured by the licensee from KSEB Ltd.</p>

<h3>Operation & Maintenance (O&M) Expenses</h3>

<p><b>34.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;Operation and Maintenance (O&M) expenses are incurred by the licensee for the day-to-day running of the distribution business. These are controllable expenses and are categorized into three main heads: Employee Costs, Repairs & Maintenance (R&M) Expenses, and Administration & General (A&G) Expenses. The Commission sets normative ceilings for these expenses for the control period, which are subject to prudence checks during the truing-up process.</p>

<h4>A. Employee Cost</h4>

<p><b>36.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;The licensee has claimed an amount of Rs. 257.53 lakh towards Employee Cost for FY 2024-25. This includes salaries, wages, dearness allowance, bonuses, staff welfare expenses, and terminal benefits for the employees engaged in the electricity distribution business. The licensee has stated that the increase in employee cost is primarily due to the impact of the triennial wage revision settlement and annual increments.</p>

<p><b>37.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;As per the KSERC (Terms and Conditions for Determination of Tariff) Regulations, 2021, employee expenses are to be governed by the normative levels approved by the Commission for the control period. The Commission had approved a normative Employee Cost of Rs. 182.75 lakh for FY 2024-25 in its MYT order. Any claim above this normative ceiling requires exceptional justification and is subject to a strict prudence check.</p>

<p><b>38.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;The Commission has examined the claim of the licensee. The claimed amount of Rs. 257.53 lakh exceeds the approved norm of Rs. 182.75 lakh by Rs. 74.78 lakh. While the licensee has cited wage revision as a reason for the increase, the MYT norms are designed to account for normal escalations. The Commission has consistently held that expenses beyond the normative ceiling for controllable items are to be borne by the licensee as a measure of efficiency. This approach is consistent with the precedent set in the Truing-Up order for KDHP for FY 2022-23, where the Commission approved Rs. 171.67 lakh against a claim of Rs. 179.80 lakh.</p>

<p><b>39.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;The Commission finds that the licensee has not provided sufficient justification to warrant an approval of expenses beyond the normative ceiling. The impact of wage revisions is an operational matter for the licensee to manage within the approved financial framework. Allowing claims beyond the norms would dilute the incentive for cost control and efficiency embedded in the regulatory framework.</p>

<h4>B. Repairs & Maintenance (R&M) Expenses</h4>

<p><b>41.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;The licensee has claimed Rs. 51.17 lakh towards Repairs and Maintenance (R&M) expenses for FY 2024-25. These expenses are incurred for the upkeep of the distribution network, including lines, cables, transformers, switchgear, and other associated equipment, to ensure reliable and safe supply of electricity to consumers.</p>

<p><b>42.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;The Tariff Regulations, 2021, provide for a normative R&M expense, which is escalated annually based on an approved inflation index. For FY 2024-25, the Commission had approved a normative R&M expense of Rs. 45.83 lakh for the licensee. The licensee's claim of Rs. 51.17 lakh is higher than this approved norm by Rs. 5.34 lakh.</p>

<p><b>43.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;The Commission has reviewed the details of the R&M expenses submitted by the licensee. The major components of the claim include expenses on transformer rewinding and maintenance of the distribution lines. While these are essential activities, the overall expenditure must be contained within the normative limits set by the Commission. The precedent from the Truing-Up order for FY 2022-23, where the Commission approved Rs. 43.06 lakh against a claim of Rs. 57.54 lakh, reinforces the principle of adhering to normative ceilings.</p>

<p><b>44.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;The Commission is of the view that the approved norm of Rs. 45.83 lakh is adequate to meet the prudent R&M requirements of the licensee's network. No specific uncontrollable factors or one-time expenses have been demonstrated by the licensee to justify the claim in excess of the norm.</p>

<p><b>45.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;<p class="commission-decision">Therefore, the Commission disallows the excess claim of Rs. 5.34 lakh and hereby approves Repairs & Maintenance (R&M) expenses of Rs. 45.83 lakh for the financial year 2024-25, as per the approved MYT norm.</p></p>

<h4>C. Administration & General (A&G) Expenses</h4>

These expenses include insurance, audit fees, legal charges, communication expenses, and other overheads necessary for the administration of the distribution business.</p>

<p><b>47.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;Similar to other O&M components, A&G expenses are governed by a normative ceiling under the Tariff Regulations, 2021. The Commission had approved a norm of Rs. 15.14 lakh for A&G expenses for the licensee for FY 2024-25. The licensee's claim exceeds this norm by Rs. 8.16 lakh.</p>

<p><b>48.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;The Commission has analyzed the breakup of the A&G expenses. The major items contributing to the higher claim are legal charges and audit fees. The Commission has consistently held that certain administrative expenses, especially those arising from litigation against the Commission's own orders, are not admissible. The approved norm is considered sufficient to cover all prudent and necessary administrative overheads. The approach is consistent with the Truing-Up order for FY 2022-23, where the Commission approved Rs. 19.37 lakh against a claim of Rs. 30.50 lakh.</p>

<p><b>49.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;The licensee has not provided any compelling justification for the Commission to deviate from the approved norm. The excess expenditure is deemed to be a result of internal cost management choices and cannot be passed on to the consumers through tariffs.</p>

<h3>Depreciation</h3>

<p><b>51.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;The licensee has claimed a depreciation of Rs. 14.50 lakh for FY 2024-25. Depreciation represents the recovery of the cost of capital assets over their useful life. It is a non-cash expense but is a critical component of the ARR, allowing the utility to fund asset replacement and modernization.</p>

<p><b>52.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;As per Regulation 27 of the KSERC (Terms and Conditions for Determination of Tariff) Regulations, 2021, depreciation is to be computed using the Straight-Line Method at the rates specified in the regulations. The depreciation is chargeable on the value of assets funded through loans and equity, while assets funded by consumer contributions or capital subsidies are not eligible for depreciation.</p>

<p><b>53.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;The Commission has re-computed the eligible depreciation based on the Gross Fixed Assets (GFA) at the beginning of the year, approved asset additions during the year, and the applicable depreciation rates. The Commission's calculation shows that an amount of Rs. 1.10 lakh claimed by the licensee pertains to assets that are not eligible for depreciation recovery under the regulations. The precedent from the FY 2022-23 order, where the Commission approved Rs. 12.18 lakh, has also been considered.</p>

<p><b>54.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;<p class="commission-decision">After a prudence check and re-computation as per the Tariff Regulations, 2021, the Commission hereby approves a depreciation of Rs. 13.40 lakh for the financial year 2024-25, against the licensee's claim of Rs. 14.50 lakh.</p></p>

<h3>Interest on Normative Loan</h3>

<p><b>55.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;The licensee has claimed an amount of Rs. 41.72 lakh towards Interest on Loan. This component of the ARR allows the licensee to recover the financing cost associated with the debt portion of its capital investments.</p>

<p><b>56.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;As per the Tariff Regulations, 2021, the interest on loan is to be computed on a normative basis. The normative loan is considered to be 70% of the approved capital expenditure. The interest rate applicable is the weighted average rate of the licensee's actual loan portfolio, or a benchmark rate (SBI Base Rate/MCLR/EBLR) if no actual loans exist. The Commission had approved a normative interest on loan of Rs. 4.97 lakh in the MYT order for FY 2024-25.</p>

<p><b>57.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;The Commission has examined the licensee's claim and finds it to be significantly higher than the approved norm. The claim appears to be based on actual interest payments which may not align with the normative debt-equity structure and approved capital expenditure as per the regulations. The Commission maintains that the recovery of financing costs must adhere to the normative principles to ensure that consumers are not burdened with the costs of an inefficient capital structure.</p>

<p><b>58.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;<p class="commission-decision">The Commission, therefore, restricts the allowable interest on loan to the MYT approved norm. Accordingly, the Commission hereby approves an amount of Rs. 4.97 lakh towards Interest on Normative Loan for the financial year 2024-25, disallowing the excess claim of Rs. 36.75 lakh.</p></p>

<h3>Other Debits</h3>

<p><b>59.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;The licensee has claimed an amount of Rs. 24.89 lakh under the head 'Other Debits'. This claim primarily includes provisions for doubtful debts and bank guarantee charges. The Commission had approved a normative amount of Rs. 0.33 lakh for this component in the MYT order, which pertains to periodical inspection charges.</p>

<p><b>60.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;The Commission has consistently held that 'provision for doubtful debts' is not an admissible expense for tariff purposes unless the debt is actually written off as bad debt after exhausting all recovery measures, as per the procedure laid down in the regulations. The licensee has not provided evidence of any such write-offs. Further, bank guarantee charges related to legal proceedings against the Commission's orders are also not considered a pass-through expense.</p>

<p><b>61.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;Based on the established regulatory principles, the Commission disallows the claim towards provision for doubtful debts and other inadmissible charges. Only the periodical inspection charges are considered for approval.</p>

<p><b>62.</b>&nbsp;&nbsp;<p class="commission-decision">Accordingly, the Commission hereby approves an amount of Rs. 0.33 lakh under Other Debits for the financial year 2024-25, against the licensee's claim of Rs. 24.89 lakh.</p></p>
