<h3>Hearing on the Petition</h3>
<p>5. &nbsp;&nbsp;The hearing on the petition was conducted at the Court Hall, Office of the Commission on 15.01.2026. Sri. Suman Ghosh, Chief Financial Officer, represented the petitioner and responded to the queries of the Commission. The main points mentioned in the petition and presentation are briefed below:</p>
<p style="margin-left: 30px;">a) The actual energy sale for the year 2024-25 is 491.10 lakh units, which is higher than the approved 448.71 lakh units, mainly due to faster than anticipated recovery in commercial segments and higher HT consumption.</p>
<p style="margin-left: 30px;">b) The energy purchased from KSEB Ltd is 548.83 lakh units at a total cost of Rs.3434.48 lakh.</p>
<p style="margin-left: 30px;">c) The actual distribution loss for the year 2024-25 is 9.52%, which is marginally higher than the previous year's loss of 9.49% and the approved target of 9.10%.</p>
<p style="margin-left: 30px;">d) The total O&M expense claimed is Rs.332.00 lakh. The licensee has submitted that the increase in employee cost to Rs.257.53 lakh is primarily due to a triennial wage revision, which is an uncontrollable expenditure. The licensee has requested the Commission to approve the additional impact of wage revision amounting to Rs.48.12 lakh (cumulative).</p>
<p style="margin-left: 30px;">e) The licensee has claimed Interest and finance charges of Rs.71.19 lakh (after regulatory adjustments), which includes interest on normative loan, interest on security deposits, bank charges, and interest on working capital.</p>
<p style="margin-left: 30px;">f) The Non-Tariff Income claimed is Rs.124.07 lakh, which includes normative interest on the opening regulatory surplus of Rs.1287.09 lakh.</p>
<p style="margin-left: 30px;">g) The petition for the truing up of accounts for the year 2024-25 is submitted with a revenue gap of Rs.18.81 lakh after considering all regulatory adjustments.</p>

<p>10. &nbsp;&nbsp;Distribution loss is a controllable parameter as per Tariff Regulations, 2021. Regulation 73(3) specifies the methodology to account for any variation between the actual and approved level of distribution loss. The relevant portions of the Regulation are as shown below:</p>
<blockquote>
  <p><b>73. Distribution loss...</b></p>
  <p>(3) Any variation between the actual level of distribution losses and the approved level of distribution losses shall be dealt with, as part of the truing up of the respective financial year in the following manner:-</p>
  <p>i. if the actual distribution loss is higher than the approved level of distribution loss for any particular financial year of the control period, then the quantum of power purchase corresponding to the excess distribution loss for that financial year, shall be disallowed at the average cost of power purchase for the respective financial year;</p>
  <p>ii. if the actual distribution loss is lower than the approved level of distribution loss for any particular financial year of the control period, then the savings in the power purchase cost corresponding to the difference in distribution loss for that financial year at the average cost of power purchase for the respective financial year, shall be shared between the distribution business/licensee and the consumers in the ratio of 2:1</p>
</blockquote>

<h4>Employee Cost</h4>
<p>15. &nbsp;&nbsp;The employee cost claimed by the licensee for the year 2024-25 is Rs.257.53 lakh against the approved norm of Rs.182.75 lakh. The licensee has stated that the increase is due to a triennial wage settlement, which is an uncontrollable factor. The Commission has consistently held that O&M expenses, including employee costs, are to be governed by the norms specified in the Tariff Regulations. Any additional expenses relating to pay revision can be considered only after a prudence check. The Commission notes that the claim is significantly higher than the normative amount. Adhering to the established regulatory principles, the Commission disallows the excess claim of Rs.74.78 lakh. Accordingly, the Commission hereby approves the employee expenses of Rs.182.75 lakh as per the norms for the year 2022-23.</p>

<h4>Repair and Maintenance Expenses</h4>
<p>16. &nbsp;&nbsp;The licensee has claimed Rs.51.17 lakh towards R&M expenses for the year 2024-25 as against the approved norm of Rs.45.83 lakh. The licensee has not provided sufficient justification for claiming expenses beyond the normative level for routine R&M activities. Therefore, the Commission disallows the excess claim of Rs.5.34 lakh. Accordingly, the Commission hereby approves R&M expenses of Rs.45.83 lakh for the year 2024-25.</p>

<h4>Administration & General Expenses</h4>
<p>17. &nbsp;&nbsp;The licensee has claimed A&G expense of Rs.23.30 lakh against the approved norm of Rs.15.14 lakh. The claim includes Rs.2.15 lakh towards a BEE-mandated energy audit fee. The Commission considers this a 'change in law' event and allows this cost over and above the norm. The remaining claim of Rs.21.15 lakh is still higher than the norm of Rs.15.14 lakh. The Commission disallows the excess claim of Rs.6.01 lakh. Accordingly, the Commission hereby approves an amount of Rs.17.29 lakh (Rs.15.14 lakh + Rs.2.15 lakh) as A&G expenses for the year 2024-25.</p>

<h3>Depreciation</h3>
<p>20. &nbsp;&nbsp;The licensee in the petition has claimed a depreciation of Rs.14.50 lakh. As per KSERC Tariff Regulation 27, depreciation is computed using the Straight Line Method. The Commission has examined the details of the Gross Fixed Assets (GFA) and the applicable rates. After disallowing depreciation on assets not eligible for recovery, the Commission finds that an amount of Rs.1.10 lakh is to be disallowed. Accordingly, the Commission hereby approves depreciation of Rs.13.40 lakh for the year 2024-25 as claimed by the licensee.</p>

<h3>Interest and finance charges</h3>
<p>21. &nbsp;&nbsp;The licensee in the petition has claimed interest and finance charges of Rs.71.19 lakh (after regulatory adjustments), which includes interest on normative loan, interest on working capital, interest on security deposit & bank charges. The Commission's analysis of each component is detailed below.</p>

<h4>A. Interest on Normative Loan</h4>
<p>22. &nbsp;&nbsp;The licensee has claimed Rs.6.09 lakh as interest on normative loan. The Commission has examined the calculation based on the opening normative loan, approved asset additions, and the applicable base rate (SBI EBLR as on 01.04.2024) as per Regulation 29 of the Tariff Regulations, 2021. The claim is found to be in order. Accordingly, the Commission approves Rs.6.09 lakh as Interest on Normative Loan.</p>

<h4>B. Interest on security deposits and Bank charges</h4>
<p>23. &nbsp;&nbsp;The licensee has claimed Rs.18.93 lakh as interest on security deposits and Rs.7.53 lakh towards bank charges. The interest on security deposits is calculated at the bank rate of 6.75% as on 01.04.2024, which is in line with the Supply Code. The claim of Rs.18.93 lakh is approved. For bank charges, the claim includes Rs.4.23 lakh towards a Bank Guarantee related to a legal proceeding. The Commission has consistently disallowed such charges. The remaining Rs.3.30 lakh for Letter of Credit for power purchase is considered a legitimate business expense. Accordingly, the Commission approves Rs.18.93 lakh for interest on security deposits and Rs.3.30 lakh for bank charges, totaling Rs.22.23 lakh.</p>

<h4>C. Interest on working Capital</h4>

As per Regulation 32 of the Tariff Regulations 2021, interest on working capital is to be computed based on approved O&M expenses, approved revenue, and cash security deposits. The interest rate is 2% above the base rate. The Commission has re-computed the eligible interest on working capital based on the approved figures in this order.</p>

<h3>Return on Net Fixed Assets</h3>
<p>27. &nbsp;&nbsp;The licensee has claimed an amount of Rs.5.63 lakh as Return on Net Fixed Assets (RoNFA). As per Regulation 28 of Tariff Regulations, 2021, return shall be allowed at 5.50 percent on the net fixed assets at the beginning of the financial year. The Commission has worked out the opening NFA as Rs.102.28 lakh. The RoNFA works out to Rs.5.63 lakh (Rs.102.28 lakh x 5.50%). The claim is in line with the regulations. Accordingly, the Commission hereby approves Return on Net Fixed Assets of Rs.5.63 lakh for the year 2024-25.</p>

<h3>Other Debits</h3>
<p>28. &nbsp;&nbsp;The licensee has claimed Rs.32.36 lakh under Other Debits, which includes Duty-III charges (Rs.24.89 lakh), provision for doubtful debts (Rs.7.14 lakh) and periodical inspection charges (Rs.0.33 lakh). The Commission has consistently disallowed Duty-III charges and provisions for doubtful debts from being passed on to consumers. Only the periodical inspection charges are admissible. Accordingly, the Commission approves Rs.0.33 lakh under this head.</p>

<h3>Revenue from Sale of Power</h3>
<p>29. &nbsp;&nbsp;The licensee in the petition has claimed an amount of Rs.3707.46 lakh as revenue for the sale of 491.10 lakh units. The Commission has already approved the sales units in paragraph 8 of this order. The revenue realization is based on the applicable tariff orders. Accordingly, the Commission hereby approves the revenue from sale of power at Rs.3707.46 lakh as claimed by the licensee for the purpose of Truing Up of Accounts for the year 2024-25.</p>

The Commission has examined the components and finds the claim to be in order. Accordingly, the Commission considers the claims and approves Rs.124.07 lakh as actual non-tariff income for the year 2024-25.</p>
