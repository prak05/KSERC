1. Introduction

1.  M/s Infopark, Kochi (hereinafter referred to as the licensee or Infopark) is a Government of Kerala Undertaking, established in 2004, engaged in the development of infrastructure for information technology (IT) and information technology enabled services (ITeS). Infopark operates its central hub in Kochi, complemented by satellite campuses in Cherthala and Koratty, creating a comprehensive network across the state. The licensee is providing ready-to-occupy infrastructure, including power distribution, to IT companies in the Kakkanad and Cherthala areas. Infopark Cherthala was granted a distribution licence vide order dated 10-05-2010 of the Commission, with electricity distribution operations commencing from 01-07-2013. The Infopark Phase II campus at Kochi was granted a distribution licence vide order dated 18-10-2011. The licensee has been continuously pursuing improvements in power quality for consumers within its licensed areas.

2.  The present petition has been filed by M/s Infopark Kerala for the truing up of accounts for the financial year 2024-25. This petition is submitted in accordance with the provisions of the KSERC (Terms and Conditions for Determination of Tariff) Regulations, 2021, which govern the tariff determination for the five-year Control Period starting from April 01, 2022, and ending on March 31, 2027. The Commission vide Order in OP No.09/2023 dated 24.01.2024 had approved the Aggregate Revenue Requirement (ARR) and Expected Revenue from Charges (ERC) for the Control Period, setting the framework for the current truing up exercise.

3.  The truing up of accounts is a crucial regulatory exercise mandated by the KSERC (Terms and Conditions for Determination of Tariff) Regulations, 2021. Regulation 3 of these Regulations outlines the scope and objectives of tariff determination, while Regulations 67 and 68 specifically address the truing up process. Regulation 67 mandates the licensee to submit a petition for truing up of accounts for each financial year within the Control Period, detailing the actual performance against the approved ARR and ERC. Regulation 68 empowers the Commission to undertake a prudence check of the actual expenses and revenues, allowing for adjustments to ensure that only prudently incurred costs are passed on to consumers and that efficiency gains are appropriately shared. This process ensures transparency, accountability, and fairness in tariff determination.

4. Distribution Loss

6.  Distribution loss is an inherent characteristic of any electricity distribution network, representing the difference between the energy input into the system and the energy billed to consumers. For Infopark, this involves measuring the total energy purchased from KSEB Ltd and any solar power injected, along with wheeled energy, against the energy sold to its various consumer categories in Kakkanad and Cherthala. Efficient management of distribution losses is crucial for the financial health of the licensee and for ensuring reliable power supply to consumers, as it directly impacts the overall cost of power and the tariff structure.

7.  The Commission, in its Multi-Year Tariff (MYT) Order for the control period 2022-27 (OP No.09/2023), had approved a target distribution loss of 4.23% for the financial year 2024-25, considering various operational factors and efficiency benchmarks. In the present petition for truing up of accounts for FY 2024-25, the licensee has claimed an actual distribution loss of 2.86%. This indicates that the licensee has achieved a distribution loss percentage lower than the approved target, signifying an efficiency gain in its distribution operations for the year.

8.  Regulation 73 of the KSERC (Terms and Conditions for Determination of Tariff) Regulations, 2021, specifies the methodology for dealing with variations in distribution losses:

Regulation 73. Distribution loss...
(3) Any variation between the actual level of distribution losses and the approved level of distribution losses shall be dealt with, as part of the truing up of the respective financial year in the following manner:-
i. if the actual distribution loss is higher than the approved level of distribution loss for any particular financial year of the control period, then the quantum of power purchase corresponding to the excess distribution loss for that financial year, shall be disallowed at the average cost of power purchase for the respective financial year;
ii. if the actual distribution loss is lower than the approved level of distribution loss for any particular financial year of the control period, then the savings in the power purchase cost corresponding to the difference in distribution loss for that financial year at the average cost of power purchase for the respective financial year, shall be shared between the distribution business/licensee and the consumers in the ratio of 2:1

9.  The total energy input into the distribution system for the year 2024-25 is 242.68 lakh units. The actual distribution loss achieved by the licensee is 2.86%, which corresponds to 6.95 lakh units. The approved target distribution loss for the year was 4.23%, which would have amounted to 10.41 lakh units (4.23% of 246.09 lakh units, where 246.09 is the sum of 139.93 KSEB energy + 102.75 wheeled energy + 3.41 solar energy from the approved energy input table). Therefore, the licensee has achieved a saving of 3.46 lakh units (10.41 lakh units - 6.95 lakh units). At an average power purchase cost of Rs. 7.31 per unit, this translates to a total saving of Rs. 25.29 lakh (3.46 lakh units Rs. 7.31/unit).

10.  As per Regulation 73(3)(ii), when the actual distribution loss is lower than the approved level, the savings in power purchase cost are to be shared between the licensee and consumers in the ratio of 2:1. Accordingly, 2/3rd of the savings, amounting to Rs. 16.86 lakh (2/3 Rs. 25.29 lakh), is to be retained by the licensee as an efficiency gain. The remaining 1/3rd of the savings is passed on to the consumers. This approach incentivizes the licensee to improve operational efficiency and reduce losses further.

11.  The Commission, after careful consideration of the licensee's submission and the provisions of the Tariff Regulations, acknowledges the efficiency gain achieved by Infopark in reducing distribution losses below the approved target. The actual distribution loss of 2.86% is approved, and the corresponding efficiency gain is accounted for as per the regulatory framework.

5. Power Purchase Cost

12.  The licensee has claimed Rs. 1023.94 lakh on account of power purchase cost for the financial year 2024-25. This amount includes the cost incurred for purchasing 139.93 lakh units of energy from KSEB Ltd. The licensee has also stated that no solar power was injected to the grid during this period, as the entire energy generated by prosumers was utilized for their internal requirements. The average power purchase cost claimed is Rs. 7.31 per unit, which is consistent with the details provided in the petition.

13.  The Commission notes that the quantum of power purchased from KSEB Ltd has increased compared to the previous year, aligning with the overall increase in energy sales and wheeled energy. The licensee's submission indicates that the increase in power purchase cost is primarily due to the increase in consumption, with no significant hike in Basic Supply Tariff (BST) during 2024-25, though Retail Supply Tariff (RST) increased marginally. The average power purchase cost of Rs. 7.31 per unit is within reasonable limits, reflecting the prevailing market conditions and approved rates.

14.  As per Regulation 73(3) of the Tariff Regulations, 2021, any variation between the actual and approved distribution loss levels impacts the power purchase cost. In this case, the actual distribution loss is lower than the approved target, resulting in an efficiency gain. This gain is shared between the licensee and consumers, with 2/3rd of the savings retained by the licensee. This mechanism ensures that the licensee is incentivized for efficient operations while also passing on benefits to consumers.

15.  The Commission has examined the details furnished by the licensee regarding power purchase units and cost, which align with the records of KSEB Ltd. The actual distribution loss is lower than the approved level, leading to an efficiency gain. The approved power purchase cost for 2024-25, considering the savings on account of reduction in distribution loss, is Rs. 1040.80 lakh.

The Commission, after analyzing the details and duly considering the efficiency gain from reduced distribution losses, hereby approves Rs. 1040.80 lakh as the power purchase cost for the year 2024-25.

6. Operation & Maintenance Expenses

6a. O&M Overview

16.  Operation and Maintenance (O&M) expenses are a critical component of the Aggregate Revenue Requirement (ARR) for any distribution licensee. These expenses are generally considered controllable and encompass three main categories: Employee Cost, Repair & Maintenance Expenses, and Administrative & General Expenses. The prudent management of these costs is essential for maintaining operational efficiency and ensuring that tariffs remain reasonable for consumers.

17.  As per Regulation 80(2) of the KSERC (Terms and Conditions for Determination of Tariff) Regulations, 2021, distribution licensees are permitted to recover O&M expenses based on the normative levels specified in Annexure 7 of the Regulations for each financial year of the Control Period. The Regulations also provide for the consideration of additional expenses related to uncontrollable factors or one-time expenditures, subject to a prudence check by the Commission during the truing-up process.

18.  For the financial year 2024-25, the Commission had approved a normative O&M expense of Rs. 56.56 lakh in the ARR&ERC Order. However, the licensee has claimed a total O&M expense of Rs. 98.22 lakh in its truing-up petition. This claimed amount is significantly higher than the approved normative level, necessitating a detailed examination and prudence check by the Commission to determine the allowable expenses.

7. Depreciation

35.  The licensee in the petition has claimed a depreciation of Rs. 47.21 lakh in the straight-line method, in line with the Tariff Regulations, 2021, for the assets created using its own funds at Infopark Phase I, Cherthala, and Infopark Phase II, Kakkanad. The Commission, while truing up the accounts for the year 2023-24, had approved a depreciation of Rs. 47.21 lakh. The capital expenditure of Rs. 4311.95 lakh at Kakkanad Phase II, which was met out of Government grant, is not eligible for depreciation. The depreciation claimed for the year 2024-25 is shown below.

36.  As per Regulation 27 of the KSERC (Terms and Conditions for Determination of Tariff) Regulations, 2021, the depreciation for the control period shall be computed in the following manner:

Regulation 27: Depreciation.—

(1) The value of the assets for the purpose of depreciation shall be the historical cost of the asset.

(2) Depreciation shall be computed annually based on the straight-line method over the useful life of the asset as specified in Appendix-I to these regulations.

(3) The salvage value of the asset shall be considered as 10% of the historical cost of the asset and depreciation shall be allowed up to 90% of the historical cost of the asset.

(4) Depreciation shall be chargeable from the month in which the asset is capitalised.

(5) Depreciation shall be allowed for assets funded by consumer contribution only if the consumer contribution is adjusted against the capital base for the purpose of return on equity.

(6) The assets funded by Government grants shall not be eligible for depreciation.

(7) In case of assets which have outlived their useful life as on the date of coming into force of these regulations, the depreciation shall be allowed at the rates specified in Appendix-I to these regulations, but limited to the extent of 90% of the historical cost of the asset.

(8) The depreciation for the assets created out of the accumulated regulatory surplus shall be allowed at the rates specified in Appendix-I to these regulations.

37.  The licensee has provided a detailed breakdown of its assets, including those at Infopark Phase I, Cherthala, and Infopark Phase II, Kakkanad. (v) In the case of distribution business/licensee the working capital shall comprise of:-
