1.   M/s Infopark, Kochi (hereinafter called the licensee or Infopark) is a Government of Kerala Undertaking, engaged in the development of infrastructure for information technology (IT) and IT enabled services. The licensee provides ready-to-occupy infrastructure, including power distribution, to IT companies in its Kakkanad and Cherthala campuses. The Commission granted a distribution licence for the Cherthala campus vide order dated 10-05-2010, with operations commencing on 01-07-2013. The Infopark Phase II campus at Kochi was granted a distribution licence vide order dated 18-10-2011.

In accordance with these regulations, the licensee filed the petition on 29-11-2025 for the truing up of its accounts for the financial year 2024-25, which is the third year of the Control Period.

Hearing on the Petition

5.   The hearing on the petition was conducted at the Court Hall, Office of the Commission on 15-02-2026. Sri. Susanth Kurunthil, CEO, and Sri. Vijayan V.R, Dy. General Manager, represented the petitioner and responded to the queries of the Commission. The main points mentioned in the presentation are briefed below:

a) The number of consumers during the year 2024-25 increased to 217, and the actual sale for the year is 137.61 lakh units.

b) There are 189 consumers in the Kakkanad campus and 28 consumers in the Cherthala campus. Sales at Kakkanad were 125.53 lakh units, while sales at Cherthala were 12.08 lakh units.

c) The total energy purchased from KSEB Ltd is 139.93 lakh units. No solar power was injected into the grid during the period.

d) The overall distribution loss for the year 2024-25 is claimed at 2.86%, with an actual power purchase cost of Rs. 1023.94 lakh. The distribution loss for Cherthala is 10.49% and for Kakkanad is 2.41%.

e) The licensee has claimed total Operation and Maintenance (O&M) expenses of Rs. 98.22 lakh, which is higher than the normative O&M of Rs. 56.56 lakh approved by the Commission for the year.

f) The Employee cost claimed for the year 2024-25 is Rs. 29.86 lakh, against the approved norm of Rs. 25.04 lakh.

g) The actual R&M expense for the year is Rs. 58.30 lakh, which includes one-time expenses for the replacement of an EHT bushing and other works. The licensee stated that the O&M contractor was selected through a competitive bidding process.

h) The A&G expense claimed for the year is Rs. 10.06 lakh, against the approved norm of Rs. 5.11 lakh. Major expenses include fees for a mandatory energy audit, insurance, and inspectorate fees.

i) The licensee has claimed Interest and finance charges of Rs. 74.35 lakh, which includes interest on normative loan, interest on security deposit, interest on working capital, and bank charges.

j) Return on Net Fixed Assets (RoNFA) of Rs. 36.11 lakh has been claimed by the licensee.

k) The total expenditure incurred in 2024-25 is Rs. 1342.09 lakh, and the total revenue is Rs. 1262.32 lakh, resulting in a claimed revenue gap of Rs. 79.76 lakh.

6.   KSEB Ltd, vide letter dated 12.02.2026, submitted its comments on the petition, noting that the energy purchase units and cost tally with its records. KSEB Ltd pointed out that the claimed O&M expenses are significantly higher than the ceiling levels specified in the Tariff Regulations, 2021, and submitted that such expenses, being controllable, should be disallowed after a prudence check.

11.   The Commission, while issuing the Order on ARR&ERC for the control period, had approved a distribution loss of 4.23% for the year 2024-25. The licensee has achieved an actual distribution loss of 2.86%, which is significantly lower than the approved target.

12.   Distribution loss is a controllable parameter as per Tariff Regulations, 2021. Regulation 73(3) specifies the methodology to account for any variation between the actual and approved levels of distribution loss. The relevant portion of the Regulation is shown below:

73. Distribution loss...

(3) Any variation between the actual level of distribution losses and the approved level of distribution losses shall be dealt with, as part of the truing up of the respective financial year in the following manner:-

...ii. if the actual distribution loss is lower than the approved level of distribution loss for any particular financial year of the control period, then the savings in the power purchase cost corresponding to the difference in distribution loss for that financial year at the average cost of power purchase for the respective financial year, shall be shared between the distribution business/licensee and the consumers in the ratio of 2:1

13.   As per Regulation 73, the reduction in distribution loss is an efficiency parameter. Considering that the licensee has achieved a distribution loss better than the approved target, the Commission approves the actual loss of 2.86%. The efficiency gain on distribution loss is to be shared in a 2:1 ratio between the licensee and consumers. The calculation of this gain is shown below.

Power purchase cost

14.   The power purchase cost claimed for the year 2024-25 is Rs. 1023.94 lakh for the purchase of 139.93 lakh units from KSEB Ltd. The actual distribution loss is lower than the approved level, resulting in savings in power purchase cost. As per the regulations, this gain is to be shared between the licensee and the consumers.

15.   The Commission notes that the sharing of efficiency gains is typically handled through an adjustment in the final revenue gap. However, following the precedent set in previous orders for the licensee, the Commission will adjust the Power Purchase Cost itself to account for the licensee's share of the gain. The share of the gain to be passed on to consumers (Rs. 4.88 lakh) will be deducted from the final revenue requirement. The approved power purchase cost is therefore calculated by adding the licensee's retainable share of the gain to the actual cost incurred.

Repair and Maintenance Expenses

21.   The licensee has claimed Rs. 58.30 lakh towards R&M expenses for the year 2024-25, against the approved norm of Rs. 26.41 lakh. The licensee has stated that the higher claim is due to one-time expenses of Rs. 19.28 lakh, primarily for the replacement of an EHT bushing at the 220 kV transformer, and an increase in the cost of the outsourced O&M contract.

22.   The Commission notes that R&M expenses are specified as a normative, controllable parameter in the Tariff Regulations. While Regulation 80(3) allows for one-time expenditure of a special nature, the items claimed by the licensee, such as bushing replacement and oil leakage arresting, are part of regular asset maintenance and do not qualify as special, unforeseen expenditure. Therefore, the claim must be limited to the normative level. The excess claim of Rs. 32.12 lakh is disallowed.

A. Interest on Normative Loan

33.   The licensee has claimed Rs. 56.33 lakh as interest on normative loan. As per Regulation 29(4) of the Tariff Regulations 2021, if the regulated business of the licensee does not have an actual loan portfolio, interest charge is to be allowed at the base rate, which is the External Benchmark-Linked Lending Rate (EBLR) of SBI as on the first day of the financial year. The Commission, in the ARR/ERC order for the control period, had approved a normative interest of Rs. 43.68 lakh for FY 2024-25. The licensee's claim exceeds this approved norm. The Commission finds no justification to deviate from the norm established for the control period. Accordingly, the Commission approves an amount of Rs. 43.68 lakh as Interest on Normative Loan for FY 2024-25.

B. Interest on working Capital

34.   The licensee has claimed an amount of Rs. 8.89 lakh as interest on working capital for the year 2024-25. As per Regulation 32 of the Tariff Regulations 2021, interest on working capital for a distribution licensee is to be computed normatively.

35.   The Commission has computed the normative working capital based on the approved O&M expenses and revenue from sale of power. The interest rate is taken as the SBI 1-year EBLR as on 01-04-2024 plus 2%, which amounts to 11.15%. The detailed calculation is as follows:

C. Interest on security deposits and Bank charges

37.   The licensee has claimed Rs. 9.13 lakh towards interest on consumer security deposits and bank charges. While acknowledging the increase in consumer base, the Commission finds the claimed amount to be excessive. After prudence check and considering a reasonable growth, the Commission approves an amount of Rs. 4.91 lakh towards Interest on security deposits and Bank charges.

D. Carrying cost for past revenue gaps

38.   The licensee has claimed a carrying cost of Rs. 62.26 lakh for the year 2024-25. This is based on the cumulative revenue gap of Rs. 915.59 lakh approved up to the year 2023-24. The rate of interest considered for computing the carrying cost is 6.80% per annum (the retail term deposit rate of SBI for a tenure of one year as on 01-04-2024).

39.   The Commission has examined the claim and finds the calculation to be in order. The carrying cost of the cumulative revenue gap is allowed to compensate the licensee for the cost of funds incurred to finance the gap. Accordingly, the Commission approves the carrying cost of Rs. 62.26 lakh (Rs. 915.59 lakh 6.80%) as claimed.

Return on Net Fixed Assets

40.   The licensee in the petition for Truing up of accounts for the year 2024-25 has claimed an amount of Rs. 36.11 lakh as Return on Net Fixed Assets (RoNFA). This has been calculated at 5.50% of the opening Net Fixed Assets (NFA) of Rs. 656.48 lakh.

41.   As per Regulation 28 of the Tariff Regulations, 2021, if the equity capital of the licensee is not clearly identifiable, the return shall be allowed at 5.50 percent on the net fixed assets at the beginning of the financial year for such regulated business. The Commission finds the calculation submitted by the licensee to be in line with the regulations.

42.   Accordingly, the Commission hereby approves a Return on Net Fixed Assets of Rs. 36.11 lakh for the year 2024-25.
